### Running the tests

The integration tests compare against the outputs stored in `test/data/config1/expected_output` with a relative tolerance, because results change slightly between compilers and flags. The measured differences behind each limit are listed in [test_integration.f90](../test/test_integration.f90). Regenerate the stored outputs only when a change is meant to alter the results.

### Free-surface image term

The Green function holds the image term 1/r1 of the free surface. Until this change it was taken at the centroid of the source panel and multiplied by the area, also for panels next to the field point, while the 1/r term was integrated over the panel. On a panel close to z = 0 compared with its size, the centroid is too close to its image: for the top row of a wall graded to the waterline, with height 0.02 m and width 0.65 m, the one-point value is 4.7 times the integral. Now, for every pair within 50 panel sizes (`FLAG = 1`), `IMAGE_TERM_EXACT` and `IMAGE_TERM_EXACT_MULTI` move the term and its gradient out of the Green function and add their integral over the panel. They are called in `CALGREEN`, `CALGREEN_IRR`, `CALGREEN_MULTI`, `CALGREEN_MULTI_IRR`, `CalPotential` and `CalPotentialMulti`, so collocation and field points are treated the same. This changes first-order results on all meshes, a little: see [the fixtures](../test/data/image_term/README.md). The stored outputs of `config1` were regenerated for it.

One-point and integrated image terms converge to the same limit at second order. On half-spheroids in deep water, from 144 to 9216 panels, with even and thin top rows, A and B from both agree with the exact limits to within 0.13% at the finest mesh, and neither is the more accurate in every mode. The integrated term makes the results depend less on the lid: at 576 panels the median change from adding one is 0.03 percentage points, against 0.08 with the one-point term.

### Dipole (zero-thickness) panels

- **Accuracy.** Constant dipole panels collocated at the centroids converge as O(h) near a free edge, where μ goes to zero like the square root of the distance. On a disc in an infinite fluid, A33 is 8.2 % above (8/3) ρ a³ (Lamb 1932, Sec. 108) at 384 panels and 4.4 % above at 1536. The flat-plate analogue in 2D has the error +1/N exactly; there, collocation at the angular midpoints of cosine-spaced panels is exact (Lifanov's discrete vortex method). `disc_infinite_fluid_added_mass` therefore checks the Richardson extrapolation of 384 and 1536 panels, which is within 0.66 %. The wave part's second derivative is limited by the Green function routines, not by the differences: against exact references, `INFGREEN3D`'s gradient is off by 1e-3 to 7e-3 (`test_green_hessian.f90`).

- **Checks.** A closed plate 0.2 to 0.02 m thick under a column of radius 1 m approaches the sheet (A33 within 0.2 % at 0.02 m, `thin_plate_converges_to_dipole`). The lid removes the column's first heave irregular frequency with the sheet present (`lid_dipole_removes_irregular`). Haskind's relation holds as closely as for the closed plate (`haskind_dipole`). The whole program gives the unit solve's A33 and B33 to 6 digits (`column_plate_end_to_end`).

- **Heave plate (validation).** The heave plate of Kurnia and Ducrozet (2023, Sec. 4.2.3): a column of radius 10 m and draft 9.9 m with a plate of radius 15 m, in deep water, 0.2–4.0 rad/s. The hull is the column wall and its bottom disc (1640 panels, 0.75 m); the sheet is the annulus 10–15 m (cases and comparison scripts are kept with the validation runs, outside the repository; runs on Snellius).

  - *Irregular-frequency lid.* With the plate as a closed body 0.1 m thick, the lid moves A33 above 3.3 rad/s by 27.6 % (262 lid panels) and 68.4 % (2265), and B55 by 114 % and 88 %, in % of the largest no-lid value. With the sheet it moves A33 by 0.6 % and 3.0 %, and B55 by 4.7 % and 4.3 %: the level of a closed plate 0.5 m thick (1.9 %, 5.0 %) and of the column alone (0.0 %, 0.9 %).

  - *Sheet against closed plate.* Without the lid, on the same 0.75 m panels, the sheet and the closed 0.1 m plate agree within 1.2 % in A11, A33, A55, B33 and B55 (± 0.03 rad/s around the column's interior modes left out).

  - *Against the references of Kurnia and Ducrozet (2023, Fig. 8).* With 7 rows of 0.71 m and the lid, the sheet is within 0.4 % of WAMIT in A33 (median, 0.2–3.2 rad/s, % of max|WAMIT|), and without fall-off above 3.3 rad/s. Refining the sheet toward its rim (7, 14 and 28 rows graded to r = 15 m) lowers A33 and A55 monotonically, by 3.5 % and 5.5 % of max|WAMIT| from 7 to 28 rows, as the O(h) error at a free edge predicts; the refined sheet lies 2.8 % below WAMIT in A33 and 2.5 % in A55, near HydroStar and Nemoh v3 in A33 and between WAMIT and Nemoh v3 in A55. WAMIT and Nemoh v3 themselves differ there by about 2 % in A33 and 6 % in A55. B33 does not change with refinement (0.2 %). Panels 1:95 at the rim (7.9 mm by 0.75 m) behave: smooth results and the same lid behaviour.

### Second-order wave loads

This section describes how the second-order code is built, so that it can be changed and extended: what each module does, the data flow, the formulation each routine implements, the stores, and the tests that pin each part. Usage is in [SecondOrder.in](input_files.md#secondorderin-optional), the files in [Output Files](output_files.md#wamit-format), and the accuracy and the run logs in [Second-order validation](second_order_validation.md).

### Modules and data flow

| [`SecondOrderInput.f90`](../src/SecondOrderInput.f90), [`SecondOrderTypes.f90`](../src/SecondOrderTypes.f90), [`SecondOrderOutput.f90`](../src/SecondOrderOutput.f90) | The keys of `SecondOrder.in` and their checks (`validate_second_order`); the shared types (`surface_geom`, `cs_points`, `first_order_state`, ...); the writers of every second-order file (`write_drift_row`, `write_field_row`, `write_motion_row`, the terms headers). |

### Moment reference point

For a free body with its centre of gravity at `XR` and no external stiffness or damping, $F^{(1)} = -\omega^2 m\,\xi$, so the difference is $-\tfrac14 m(\omega_2^2 - \omega_1^2)\,\xi_1 \times \xi_2^*$ under `KERNEL_DIFF`, and the same with $\xi_2$ for `KERNEL_SUM` (unit test `moment_transfer_free_body`). It is zero for the mean drift but not for the QTFs. On OC3 Hywind (Nemoh v3.0.3 test case 11) it reached 80 % of the largest pitch QTF near the heave resonance; with `moving`, HAMS-MREL agrees with Nemoh and HydroStar there. With the centre of gravity away from `XR`, external stiffness or damping, or several bodies, the mean drift moments change as well. Tests: `moving_point_moments` (near field, both kernels), `moment_origin_files` (files and motion), and the input test.

### Potential part (body forcing)

The body forcing of the second-order potential in the difference QTF (`qtf_terms = body`). Usage is in [QTFs, potential part](input_files.md#qtfs-potential-part), the files in [Output Files](output_files.md#wamit-format), and the validation in [Second-order validation](second_order_validation.md#qtf-potential-part).

| [`src/SharpEdge.f90`](../src/SharpEdge.f90) | Sharp edges of the hull and the product weights of the panels next to them, for `qtf_edge_weights = on` (off by default). |

with n_o out of the control volume on C and F (the opposite of `cs_points%nrm`). No line integral enters. H needs no ∇ψ and is singular like u only; ∇ψ on C and F is smooth. R and L stay on the hull. One rigid A per control volume, so each C must enclose its own body only, and must have a bottom: a C that reaches the sea bed would leave the sea bed as a boundary of the volume, whose term is not implemented. Moving L to C ∪ F as well, and R, was tried on the exact reference and not kept ([validation](second_order_validation.md#tried-and-not-kept)).

**Threads.** `fields_green` ([FieldVelocity.f90](../src/FieldVelocity.f90)) runs its loop over the points in parallel: each point writes only its own values, with no reduction, so the result does not depend on the thread count (unit test `green_field_thread_invariant` in `test/test_field_velocity.f90`). The panel loop of `field_influence_row` inside it is also an OpenMP loop and runs serially there while nested parallelism is off, the default. With nested parallelism on, the threads multiply (up to `NTHREAD`²); nothing guards against that. The timings are in [the run details](second_order_validation.md#measurements-behind-the-implementation).

- HAMS's panel centroids are vertex averages. On strongly trapezoidal panels they leave a large O(h²) constant in a nearly cancelling closed-surface integral, so the unit test of the Stokes form of B2 uses area centroids (`test/test_qtf_potential.f90`). Whether the vertex-average centroids of the cylinder meshes' bottom rings add a constant of this kind to their results was not measured.

- u at the waterline: the line term L with u extrapolated along a differenced panel is only O(h). `waterline_velocity` builds u at each waterline segment from the incident wave (exact), the free-surface condition (u_z), the body condition (normal part) and differencing of φ along the waterline (tangential part); B2 is then O(h²) (`test_qtf_potential.f90`). This was measured on a regular cylinder only; an irregular spacing of the waterline segments was not measured.

- The tangential differencing of φ along the waterline runs across sharp waterline corners, which makes it O(h) next to the corners of a box barge [derived, not measured]. Flared (sloped) waterline panels are not covered by a test.

- The unit tests pin the signs of the terms as specified; the physical signs are checked only against the exact reference (`truncated_cylinder_reference.py qtf-potential`), whose signs are derived from the boundary-value problem.

### Free-surface integral (`qtf_terms = full`)

- FS_inner is `fs_inner_pair`, the panel sum over the inner free-surface mesh. FS_outer is `fs_outer_pair` from R to `fs_outer_rfar` and its tail, plus `fs_outer_sliver` over the gap between the polygon of a generated inner mesh and the circle. A mesh from `FSInnerMesh.pnl` has no sliver; `fs_coverage_check` checks that it covers the free surface inside R to 1% (warning) and 10% (stop).

- **Gravity.** The free-surface terms take `s%g`, the gravity G of `Const_mod`. This is the g of the solver's free-surface condition and of the ψ solve. The hull Froude-Krylov term of φ_I2 takes `wave_gravity` (g_wave), as do the incident fields at the inner points (`incident_at`). `wave_gravity` is ω²/(k tanh kh) of the wave number from DISPERSION, which uses the same G, so the two are equal to rounding. (Before DISPERSION took `Const_mod`'s G it had g = 9.807, and the two differed by about 0.004%.)

- **The default inner mesh** was kept after the validation on the sharp cylinder ([Second-order validation](second_order_validation.md#qtf-free-surface-integral)). The log notes the mesh on every run with the generated mesh.

### The near-waterline fit of the inner fields

`fs_inner_fields` gives φ, ∇φ and φ_zz at the inner points from the panel solution, with ∇φ and φ_zz by a 5-point stencil whose offsets `fs_inner_steps` shrinks until the stencil clears the waterplanes by 16 offsets. Next to a waterline the offsets reach millimetres, below the scale on which the field of constant panels is smooth, and the differences amplify the panel-scale error, while φ itself stays accurate. At high Ω⁺ that strip carries a large share of the free-surface term ([Second-order validation](second_order_validation.md#the-free-surface-part-and-the-near-waterline-fit)). The fit replaces the gradient and φ_zz there.

- **Error floor.** The extrapolation gain of the weights is 2 at s = d0 and grows as s tends to 0 (cubic, six samples), so the stencil error at the samples sets the error left next to the waterline (values in [the run details](second_order_validation.md#measurements-behind-the-implementation)).

### Direct method

The direct solution of the difference-frequency second-order potential with body forcing (`qtf_direct = 1`) and, with `qtf_terms = full`, with the free-surface forcing ([below](developer_documentation.md#free-surface-forcing-of-the-direct-solve)). The sum frequency is [further below](developer_documentation.md#sum-frequency-of-the-direct-method). Usage is in [QTFs, potential part](input_files.md#qtfs-potential-part), the file in [Output Files](output_files.md#wamit-format), and the validation in [Second-order validation](second_order_validation.md#direct-method-against-indirect-method).

**B2 by the Stokes form with G.** B2 = −n·[(X·∇)u] has the same sharp-edge problem as in the potential part, so the Stokes form of [B2](developer_documentation.md#potential-part-body-forcing) is used with G(x_i, ·) in place of ψ. G(x_i, ·) is singular at x_i on S: ∇_ξ G goes like 1/ρ², with a leading part odd in the direction, so the surface term is a principal value and the identity keeps its form [derived, QTFDirect.f90 header]. One point per panel for the density on the 1/ρ² kernel converges slowly at collocation points on S (though at O(h²) off the surface). So on the panels that `CALGREEN` takes as near (within 50 panel sizes) the Rankine part 1/r + 1/r₁ is integrated against a density linear on the panel, with closed-form first moments over flat panels (`rankine_moments`); the density gradient comes from `tangential_fit`. The wave part and the far panels keep the one-point rule. Panels that share a node with a panel across a sharp edge (normals more than `qtf_edge_angle` apart) keep the one-point rule, because u is singular there and the fitted gradient means nothing. The mask is built once per run (`direct_setup`).

**Solve.** At each Ω the matrix is the radiation matrix of `ASSB_LEFT` (LU factors with `ISOLV = 1`). With `ISOLV = 2` it is left unfactored for GMRES, and `direct_factor` factors it once per Ω (the direct solve is its last use at that Ω; a second factorisation would give a wrong φ_D with no error, so `direct_factor` records the Ω and stops on a repeat). The right sides of all pairs of one Ω are one block, `ZGETRS` with `NRHS` = the number of pairs, per symmetry block, in blocks of at most `DIRECT_BLOCK_BYTES` (256 MB) of work arrays. A pair with Ω < 0 is treated as `potential_pair` treats it: φ_D solves the problem at −|Ω|, whose matrix and Green function are the conjugates of those at |Ω|. The equal-frequency pairs (Ω = 0) need no solve: `.12d` equals `.10d` there, as `.11d` does.

**Tests.** Unit tests in `test/test_qtf_direct.f90` (collected as `qtf_direct`; right side, B2 forms, solve, lid), `test/test_qtf.f90` (`qtf_direct_omegas_all`, `qtf_direct_missing`) and `test/test_second_order_input.f90` (`qtf_direct_key`, `qtf_direct_needs_body`). Integration tests in `test/test_integration_drift.f90`: `run_qtf_direct_cases` and `run_qtf_direct_sharp_m2` make the runs; `qtf_direct_file_layout`, `direct_vs_indirect_round`, `direct_vs_indirect_sharp`, `direct_half_matches_full`, `direct_two_bodies`, `direct_diagonal`, `direct_gmres` (`ISOLV = 2`), `direct_irr_runs`, `direct_vs_indirect_irr_file` and `direct_no_lid_stops` check the file, the agreement with `.11d`, the symmetry images, two bodies, the diagonal, the LU-per-Ω path, the lid and the stops. The limits are measured values with a margin; the printed values are in the test output.

### Free-surface forcing of the direct solve

With `qtf_terms = full` the right side of the direct solve gets the free-surface forcing of φ_D. Usage is in [QTFs, potential part](input_files.md#qtfs-potential-part), the columns in [Output Files](output_files.md#wamit-format), the validation in [Second-order validation](second_order_validation.md#direct-method-with-the-free-surface-forcing).

- *Inner* (the generated mesh or `FSInnerMesh.pnl`, nfs panels): r_in(x_i) = Σ_j gfs(i, j) w_j Q_D(ξ_j), with Q_D constant per panel (the one-point rule of `fs_inner_pair`) and gfs(i, j) = (1/w_j) ∬_panel j G(x_i, ξ) dS. A panel within 50 panel sizes of x_i takes the Rankine part 1/r + 1/r₁ in closed form (`rankine_moments`) and the rest G − 1/r − 1/r₁ by an n × n Gauss-Legendre rule on the panel, n = ⌈ln(1e8)/(2 ln ρ)⌉ with ρ = t + √(t²+1), t the distance over the panel radius, n from 1 to 8 (`fs_remainder_order`). One point (the rule of `CALGREEN`) is not enough there, since the rest is not smooth on the panel scale next to the waterline (a logarithm) and in finite depth (bottom images). A panel further away takes G at its centroid.

**Orders.** The m-th Graf term at r ≥ R is at most (r_x/R)^m/m (A&S 9.3.1), so M = `pw_green_mmax(r_max, R, 1e-8)`, r_max the largest horizontal distance of any row point from the centre. The n-th evanescent term is of order e^{−k_n(R − r_x)}, with k_n > (n − ½)π/h, which gives N_ev = ⌈L h/(πd) + ½⌉, L = ln(1/(1e-8 (1 − e^{−πd/h}))), d = R − r_max. N_ev grows like 1/d (a row at 0.9 R needs about 200). Both are capped where k_n R exceeds `PW_KR_MAX`, with a warning. `pw_green_modes` zeroes the orders whose H_{m+1}(k₀R) or K_{m+1}(k_n R) exceed 1e300 and returns the highest kept, `mcut`. Only |m| ≤ mcut is contracted, so there is never Inf × 0. If mcut < M, the dropped tail is at most q^(mcut+1)/((mcut+1)(1 − q)), q = r_max/R (`fs%cut_error`); above 1e-8 it is a warning, not a stop. The bound is large when R is close to the hull and the wave is long; the cure is a larger `qtf_fs_radius`.

**Cost and memory.** Per Ω, `direct_fs_setup` builds gfs (rows × inner panels) and the amplitudes a((2M+1) × (N_ev+1) per row): 16 · rows · (nfs + (2M+1)(N_ev+1)) bytes, which the log gives. The near-panel rule costs up to 64 Green function calls per near pair, and nearly every pair is near. For 6000 rows, M = 57 and N_ev = 75 the amplitudes take about 0.8 GB and gfs about 0.5 GB [derived]. The orders and the size do not depend on Ω, so `body_terms_setup` logs them with `direct_fs_estimate` before the frequency loop and adds them to the memory estimate; N_ev grows like h/(R − r_max), and the allocations of `direct_fs_setup` stop with a message that names a larger `qtf_fs_radius`. Image rows could share base-row storage (a_mirror(m) = a(−m)); this is not done. Per pair the moments dominate and the contraction is small. The pairs run one after another; only the radial annulus is parallel, with fixed blocks summed in order, so the bits do not depend on the thread count. Measured times: [run details](second_order_validation.md#runs-difference-qtf-direct-method-with-the-free-surface-forcing).

### Sum frequency of the direct method

`qtf = 2` or `3` with `qtf_direct = 1` and `qtf_terms = body` or `full` solves φ_D at the sum frequencies Ω = ω_i + ω_j and writes `QTF_Direct.12s` and `QTF_DirectTermsSum.txt`. Usage is in [QTFs, potential part](input_files.md#qtfs-potential-part), the files in [Output Files](output_files.md#wamit-format), the numbers in [Second-order validation](second_order_validation.md#direct-method-at-the-sum-frequency).

**The list of frequencies.** `qtf_omega_list(omegas_main, max_dw, sum_top, tol, want_diff, want_sum, need, reuse_index, is_diff, is_sum)` ([QTFDriver.f90](../src/QTFDriver.f90)) builds the union of the difference frequencies and, with `want_sum`, the sums ω_i + ω_j (i ≤ j) up to `sum_top`, merging values within tol/2. `is_diff(m)` and `is_sum(m)` say which kernels use `need(m)`; a frequency that is both (0.6 from 0.9 − 0.3 and 0.3 + 0.3) has one solve. ψ, ∇ψ on C and F and ψ on the inner free surface are kept at every entry. `qtf_radiation_list` is a wrapper with `want_sum = .FALSE.` and returns the old list to the bit (`omega_list_diff_unchanged`, three grids and two `qtf_max_dw`). `qtf_sum_limits` gives `sum_top`, the smaller of `qtf_max_sw` and the cutoff of the irregular frequencies (a hair below it, since a sum at the cutoff is left out). `sum_pair_index(i, j)` is the rule of the sum as `difference_pair_index` is that of the difference: 0 when ω_i + ω_j is beyond the cutoff, else its index in the list. `qtf_max_sw` is inclusive (to 1e-9) like `qtf_max_dw`; the estimate is exclusive. A sum-only Ω has no difference pair, so the difference direct solve skips it (`qtf_diff_omega`), but its radiation-only solve runs.

**The inner mesh and the stencil for the sum.** The Green matrix and the amplitudes are built per Ω, so only the mesh and the mode counts follow the sum. `fs_setup(..., omega_sum_max)` calls `build_fs_inner` at max(ω_max, Ω⁺_max), so the generated inner mesh has the panel size λ(Ω⁺_max)/10 (16 times the panels in deep water); one mode count M = ⌈K(Ω⁺_max) R⌉ + 10 serves all partial-wave sets. The stencil offsets of φ_zz keep the first-order scale, `scale = max(psize, λ(ω_max)/10)` in `fs_inner_steps(..., scale)`, because the first-order fields vary on λ(ω) and a smaller offset raises the rounding error by 1/δ². The scale applies only when the generated mesh was refined for the sum (no `qtf_fs_panel`, no `FSInnerMesh.pnl`), so that `qtf = 3` difference files stay equal to `qtf = 1`. `fs_inner_steps_scale` checks it. The inner mesh must be built for the largest sum frequency: a mesh built for a lower frequency gives a clearly larger gap to the indirect sum (values in [the run details](second_order_validation.md#measurements-behind-the-implementation)).

**Cost.** `direct_fs_estimate` and `qtf_stored_mb` print the sizes before the frequency loop. A sum run has 2N − 1 sum frequencies on a uniform N-point grid and N(N + 1)/2 pairs per heading pair, against N − 1 and N(N − 1)/2 for the difference, and its inner mesh is finer. The cost table and the measured times are in [the run details](second_order_validation.md#runs-sum-qtf-direct-method).

**Known limit.** At the Ω⁺ of the sum a hull in deep finite-depth water reaches ν h above 50, where the field series of `FINGREEN3D` changes regime and the log warns once (`QTF second-order free surface warning`). The measured error of `FINGREEN3D` there is in [Second-order validation](second_order_validation.md#measurements-behind-the-implementation).

### Second-order motion

`qtf_motion = 1` solves the second-order motion ξ⁽²⁾ of the free bodies at every row of `.11d` and `.11s`, and with `qtf_direct = 1` of `.12d` and `.12s`, and writes `QTF_Motion.11d`, `.11s`, `.12d` and `.12s`. With `qtf_field = 1` and `qtf_direct = 1` it also adds the radiated wave of that motion to `QTF_Field.txt`. Usage is in [Second-order motion](input_files.md#second-order-motion), the files in [Output Files](output_files.md#wamit-format), the numbers in [Second-order validation](second_order_validation.md#second-order-motion).

**The solve per Ω.** `motion_matrix(omega, mass, a, b, b_ext, c, k_ext, lhs)` returns the matrix of (1). `motion_solve_omega(lhs, forces, xi, ok, rcond)` factors a copy of it with `ZGETRF` after `ZLANGE` gives its 1-norm, estimates the reciprocal condition number with `ZGECON` (LAPACK Users' Guide, 3rd ed., Anderson et al. 1999, section 4.4), and solves every force column of that Ω with one `ZGETRS`. If `ZGETRF` finds an exactly zero pivot, rcond is set to 0. `ok = rcond >= 1e-12`; when not ok, ξ = 0 and the driver leaves the pairs of that Ω out of the files. The matrix is not equilibrated: the singular cases (free surge, sway and yaw) have rcond exactly 0, so equilibration would not change which Ω are flagged. One `ZGETRS` on all columns differs from one call per column only by rounding (MKL takes other kernels for one right side; unit test `motion_columns_independent`).

**Signs of Ω.** A difference row (i, j) with ω_i < ω_j has Ω < 0. A and B are even in Ω, since the radiation force of a real motion has conjugate coefficients at ±Ω; with M, C, K_ext and B_ext real the matrix at −|Ω| is then conj(lhs) of the one at |Ω|. `qtf_motion_part` solves lhs conj(ξ) = conj(F) with the factors of |Ω| and conjugates the result, so each Ω has one factorisation for both signs. **Ω = 0** (the difference diagonal) is static: `motion_matrix` at Ω = 0 with A = B = 0 is C + K_ext. For a singular Ω, `free_modes` names the modes whose row or column is zero to 1e-12 of the largest entry (surge, sway and yaw of an unmoored body); `omega_left_text` writes them into the log line and the file header.

**Python.** `truncated_cylinder_reference.py qtf-motion` solves (1) for surge, heave and pitch of the sharp cylinder from the exact T, A and B, with the springs and dampers of the validation case, and `qtf_compare.py --motion` compares the files with it ([Python Scripts](python_scripts.md)). The reference keeps the M and C of the exact circle, slightly above those of the HAMS polygon.

### Partial second-order field

`build_field_points` turns the points into a `cs_points` set of kind `CS_FREE`, so that `fields_green` (`FieldVelocity.f90`) computes φ at every point. Its rules: |z| ≤ 1e-6 is an elevation point (z set to 0), z < −1e-6 a pressure point, z > 1e-6 rejected; a point within `psize(i)` of a hull centroid i is on the hull; a point is inside body b when the winding number of body b (`body_winding`) is more than 0.5. It sums the solid angles (Van Oosterom and Strackee 1983, IEEE Trans. Biomed. Eng. 30(2), 125–126) of the triangles of the hull panels, symmetry images included, and of their mirror in z = 0, each turned to face out of the body; the hull and its mirror form a closed surface for a surface-piercing body, and the mirror of a submerged body lies above z = 0. An earlier rule, the side of the nearest centroid's panel plane, stopped valid points below the hull near an edge where fine wall panels meet a coarse bottom (unit test `points_mixed_panel_sizes`). The points are checked in order, and the first that fails gives the message. `psize` and `body` of a point are those of the nearest hull panel; the finite-difference step is `FIELD_FD_STEP` = 0.02 of that size.

### Field-pass speed-ups

Five changes make the field passes of `qtf_terms = full` and `qtf_b2 = control` cheaper (the free-surface fields, the C/F fields, the ψ passes). Comments in the source and the tests cite them as "item N of the field-pass speed-ups"; the numbers are in the table. The keys are in the [input files](input_files.md); the accuracy gate and the measured effect are in [Field-pass speed-ups](second_order_validation.md#field-pass-speed-ups) of the validation document.

- **The inner fields from the source strengths (`qtf_fs_inner = source`, off).** `fs_inner_fields_source` forms φ and ∇φ at the inner points from the source strengths, one row per point, without φ_zz; `fs_inner_pair_ibp` and `fs_line_pair` move the φ_zz part of the inner integral to the boundary by Green's first identity (an area part and a line integral over the waterline and the outer edge). `fs_source_sigma` solves for the strengths per state (or reuses `cs_sigma`), and `fs_gpsi` stores ∇ψ. It stops with `qtf_direct = 1`, `IRSP = 1` and `ISOLV = 2` (`validate_second_order`), and with an inner mesh that does not close on the waterline (`fs_waterline_closure`, tolerance `FS_CLOSE_TOL` = 1e-6 segment lengths; it checks the inner edge only, so a mesh that overshoots into the hull and still has edges on the waterline would pass). It is off because it failed the accuracy gate. Tests: `inner_ibp_identity`, `waterline_normal_derivative`, `fs_pair_terms_ibp_order`, `fs_inner_source_matches_exact`, `fs_inner_mesh_closure`, `qtf_fs_inner_key`, `qtf_fs_inner_source_rejections`, `qtf_stored_mb_gpsi`, and `fs_inner_source_close` in the integration suite. Two checks are open before the source route can ever be switched on: ψ_k and ∇_h ψ_k by the source route were never compared with exact data, and the reuse of `cs_sigma` (with `cs_velocity = source`) has no fixture.

### Iterative Solvers

The `ACA_TOL` and `ITER_TOL` constants are both `1e-4`, matched to the ~`1e-7` per-entry precision floor of an `AMAT` assembled from single-precision Green's-function values. Iterative results agree with the direct-LU reference at 4–5 significant figures on physically meaningful entries. For bit-identity against WAMIT references, users should stay on the default (`GRN_KIND = 8`, `ISOLV = 1`).

Small multi-body test cases with `ISOLV = 2` can be added to `test/integration_tester.f90`. The recommended check is that `ISOLV = 2` output agrees with an `ISOLV = 1` baseline to `1e-3` relative on the WAMIT `.1` (`AmssDamp.1`) file peak-magnitude entries.

### Lid mesh requirements

- **All nodes on z = 0 exactly.** The reader stops on any lid node with |z| > 1e-10 (`ReadWTPLMesh`, `ReadWTPLMeshMulti`).

Why the distance matters: on a column with a heave plate (waterline rows 0.68 m high, 832 hull panels), a lid made by clipping a square grid to the waterline circle left thin triangles at the wall, with centroids 2 to 5 cm from it. With that lid, heave damping and heave excitation between 2 and 4 s were off from an independent reference solution by 68% and 30% on average. Without a lid they were off by 12% and 9%, because of the irregular frequency in that band. A lid of three rings on the 32 waterline nodes, with outer centroids 0.38 m from the wall, brought them to 5% and 0.7%. At the zero- and infinite-frequency limits both lids gave the no-lid added mass to within 0.04%, and between 5 and 30 s to within 0.3% on added mass and 1.1% on damping.

### DipoleMesh.pnl (optional, single body)

Thin plates, such as heave plates and fins, can be given as sheets of zero thickness instead of closed bodies. Each panel of `DipoleMesh.pnl` carries the jump of the potential across the sheet, μ = φ(+n side) − φ(−n side), instead of a potential. This is a normal-dipole sheet (Hess and Smith 1964, Sec. 4.2; the dipole panels of the WAMIT User Manual). A sheet has no interior, and there are no faces close together. Close faces matter: in the heave-plate lid study of 2026-11-24 (IRSP = 1, panels 0.75 m wide), the lid moved the heave added mass above 3.3 rad/s by 27 % for a closed plate 0.1 m thick, by 2 % for one 0.5 m thick, and by 0.0 % for the column alone.

- a dipole centroid lies within 1e-6 panel sizes of a hull centroid (a sheet must not coincide with hull panels);

A sheet may meet the hull along an edge, for example a heave-plate annulus at the bottom of a column. Nothing is added at the junction. Example, the heave plate of Kurnia and Ducrozet (2023, Sec. 4.2.3), a column of radius 10 m and draft 9.9 m with a plate of radius 15 m: `HullMesh.pnl` holds the column wall down to z = −9.9 m and the column's bottom disc (r < 10 m) at z = −9.9 m; `DipoleMesh.pnl` holds only the annulus 10 m ≤ r ≤ 15 m at z = −9.9 m. The bottom disc and the annulus lie in one plane and do not overlap. Grade the annulus toward its free outer rim. On this plate, with 0.75 m panels around, the added mass in heave fell by 3.5 % of the peak of the WAMIT curve from 7 to 28 radial rows (7, 14, 28 rows with radii r = 10 + 5 sin(π i / 2N) m), and by about 1 % from 14 to 28; outer rows 1:95 (7.9 mm by 0.75 m) were fine. The sheet replaces a closed plate 0.1 m thick; without the lid the two agree within 1.2 % in A11, A33, A55, B33 and B55 on the same panel size (see the [developer documentation](developer_documentation.md#dipole-zero-thickness-panels)). At a free edge μ goes to zero like the square root of the distance to the edge (Lamb 1932, Sec. 108, for a disc). Constant panels, collocated at their centroids, converge only as O(h) there. For a disc of radius a, A33 is 13 % above the exact (8/3) ρ a³ with 96 panels, 8.2 % above with 384, 4.4 % with 1536 and 2.7 % with 3072 (6, 12, 24 and 48 rings). Grading the rings toward the rim (r_i = a sin(π i / 2N)) changes this little. So use fine sheet meshes, check a refined mesh, and, where one number is needed, extrapolate: 2 A(fine) − A(coarse), with the fine mesh refined twice in each direction, was within 0.7 % for the disc. See the [developer documentation](developer_documentation.md#dipole-zero-thickness-panels).

The depth guard of `field_deep_kh`: the deep-water rows are used only where the depth is at least this many times the draft of the hull (its deepest node, all bodies together). In shallower water the free-surface passes keep the finite-depth Green function at every frequency, and the log says so once ("the finite-depth Green function is kept from k0 h = ..."). Reason: in water shallow relative to the draft the deep-water function misses the seabed by more than k0 h accounts for. On the sharp cylinder (radius 10 m, draft 10 m, fixed body; semi-analytical reference at each depth) the deep-water rows at k0 h >= 10 moved the free-surface part of the sum QTF, F_z, from 0.72 % to 7.29 % of the largest exact value at h = 25 m (2.5 drafts) and from 0.74 % to 2.96 % at h = 50 m (5 drafts); both fail the accuracy gate (at most 0.5 percentage points), and with finite-depth rows the error stays below 0.75 %. At h = 100 m (10 drafts) the same rows pass it (largest change 0.14 percentage points at k0 h = 5). So the default is 10 drafts, the shallowest depth measured to pass; between 5 and 10 drafts it was not measured. Sources: `validation-runs/fs-speedups/deep_inner/findings.md`. `0` switches the guard off. A negative value stops the run. |

- `qtf = 2` with `qtf_direct = 0` is no longer an error: since Phase 6b it gives `QTF_Indirect.11s` (the check was removed from `SecondOrderInput.f90`).

A key that does not apply is not an error: `qtf_b2` without `qtf_terms = body` or `full` and `qtf` 1, 2 or 3, the `qtf_fs_*` keys (also `qtf_fs_panel`) without `qtf_terms = full` and `qtf` 1, 2 or 3, and `qtf_edge_weights` or `qtf_edge_angle` without `qtf_b2 = control`, are ignored, and the log says so (`QTF note: ... ignored`).

### Methods and outputs

The defaults `velocity_method = differencing` and `waterline_method = extrapolate` were kept after the [convergence study](second_order_validation.md#mean-drift-convergence-study). `differencing` is the closer to far-field on hulls without sharp edges: within 0.24% from 0.70 to 1.80 rad/s on a free rounded truncated cylinder at 7680 panels, where `source` is off by 0.12% to 2.3%. There is no switch of method by frequency.

### Which method to use

- **Rounded hulls** (no sharp edges at the bilge or corners): near-field with the defaults, for the loads per body and for F_z, M_x, M_y. Measured on a rounded truncated cylinder, a rounded box and a bottom-mounted cylinder.

  - F_z, M_x, M_y: use `control`. On a sharp truncated cylinder its F_z is within 2.3% of a [semi-analytical reference](second_order_validation.md#semi-analytical-reference-sharp-truncated-cylinder) at 4032 panels and 1.4% at 7168, fixed and free. Near-field F_z is 12% to 17% off there on the fixed hull and 23% to 506% on the free hull, with the wrong sign at 0.70 rad/s.

  - Free sharp hull at low frequency: use far-field for F_x. At 0.70 rad/s near-field F_x has the wrong sign with `differencing` at 7168 panels, and `source` is 10% off.

  - Sharp box: near-field F_y and M_z are not reliable with either velocity method on the meshes tested (F_y +12.5% with `source` at 7040 panels).

- **Hulls with slender members through the waterline**, such as the OC4 semisubmersible with its inclined cross braces: check every hull velocity against far-field. On OC4 at 5022 panels (deep water, 0.8 to 1.85 rad/s), neither velocity method met the far-field check for F_x. Near-field F_x was off by up to 7.6% with `differencing` and up to 8.8% with `source`, with opposite signs at most frequencies. `control` with `cs_velocity = source` was +2% to +10% off, since it uses the same source solve. `control` with `cs_velocity = green` met the check: within 0.8% with C and F panels of about 1 m, and 0.3% at 0.5 m. With 3.3 m panels it was off by up to 8%. On such hulls use `control` with `cs_velocity = green` and C panels of 1 m or less, and check its F_x against far-field before reading F_z.

- **`source`** costs about twice the run time (+98% at 7680 panels, ifx, 32 threads) and cannot remove irregular frequencies: `IRSP = 1` is rejected, and at the two irregular frequencies tested it is 32% to 198% off. Keep away from them, or use `differencing` with a lid there.

- **Settings:** `GRN_KIND = 8` changed no printed digit of F_x. `ISOLV = 2` (GMRES) matches LU to within 0.003 in F_x / (ρg) on the cases tried.

The evidence is in [Second-order validation](second_order_validation.md).

### Mesh guidance

- Refine towards the waterline. Thin top rows converge now that the free-surface image term is integrated over the panel ([Developer Documentation](developer_documentation.md#free-surface-image-term)): on a rounded cylinder on springs at 1.10 rad/s, near-field is +1.36%, +0.40%, +0.21% from far-field at 480, 1920, 4320 panels.

- Round corners and the bilge where the real hull allows. On a box, that took near-field against far-field from 23.4% (sharp, 7040 panels) to 0.41% (rounded, 8000 panels) for the force vector at 1.00 rad/s. It makes a different hull: judge it against its own far-field.

- If near-field is needed on a sharp edge, grade the rows strongly towards the edge. With `differencing`, rows of size (i/n)^4 at the edge (and quadratic at the waterline) took fixed near-field F_z on the sharp cylinder from 15% to 3.1% at 4032 panels, converging at about order 1.5 against 0.7 with cosine rows. This does not help `source`, and the free hull near heave resonance stays far off: see [graded meshes](second_order_validation.md#near-field-on-meshes-graded-towards-a-sharp-edge).

- Irregular frequencies: with `differencing`, use `IRSP = 1` with a lid near them. On one cylinder at 0.95 and 1.10 rad/s, a lid with thin rim panels moved near-field F_x by at most 0.42% against `IRSP = 0`.

- Control surface: the defaults of `cs_*` were used in all the studies. For small hulls in a multi-body case, a finer C (`cs_panel_size` half the default) took the sum over two bodies from 2.28% to 0.58% of far-field; see [the fixtures](../test/data/drift_cyl/README.md#control-surface-method).

### Limits

- **`waterline_method = field` in finite depth:** term I has been seen to jump with the mesh or the offset: on 32 x 16 rows at depth 10 and offset 0.01, -11.40% at 1.0 rad/s against -1.93% and -2.06% at offsets 0.005 and 0.05 (see [the fixtures](../test/data/drift_cyl/README.md)). That was measured before the finite-depth Green function used Linton's method for R/h < 0.02 (v2.3.3), which replaces the series behind such jumps for ν·h ≤ 50. It has not been measured again since. The run logs a warning when this method is used in finite depth.

- **Control surface, F_z, M_x, M_y:** validated with `cs_shape = cylinder` only, and on finer meshes at 0.70 to 1.10 rad/s only. Not computed when C reaches the sea bed or the free surface between the hull and C cannot be built (the log says why).

- **QTFs:** the quadratic part comes from the near-field method. With `qtf_terms = body` the difference QTF also has the body forcing of the second-order potential (as Nemoh's HASBO), but not the free-surface integral (Nemoh's HASFS, the free-surface part of WAMIT's indirect method; WAMIT v7.5 user manual, ch. 15), so `QTF_Indirect.11d` is incomplete. With `qtf_terms = full` (finite depth) it has the free-surface integral too and is the complete difference QTF of the indirect method; validated on one hull, the sharp cylinder, single body, heading 0 ([validation](second_order_validation.md#qtf-free-surface-integral)). The sum-frequency QTF has the quadratic part, and with `qtf_direct = 1` and `qtf_terms = full` also the second-order potential by the direct method (`QTF_Direct.12s`, the sum-frequency paragraph of [the potential part](input_files.md#qtfs-potential-part)); an indirect sum-frequency QTF is Phase 6b. See [QTFs](input_files.md#qtfs-quadratic-part) and [the potential part](input_files.md#qtfs-potential-part).

- **QTF potential part:** validated against an exact reference on one hull, the sharp truncated cylinder, heading 0, in finite depth, single body, free with and without external damping ([validation](second_order_validation.md#qtf-potential-part)). `qtf_b2 = control` was run there at 448, 1792 and 4032 panels with the default C, and at 1792 and 4032 with C fixed; at 4032 panels the two give the same accuracy. On a second hull, the columns of the OC4 semisubmersible, it was compared with Nemoh at 1224 and 4608 panels and with a public WAMIT run at both, with no exact reference. Multi-body and half-mesh runs are covered by integration tests only.

- **Second-order motion (`qtf_motion`):** validated against an exact reference on one hull, the free sharp truncated cylinder with springs in surge, sway and yaw, heading 0, depth 100 m, at two meshes ([validation](second_order_validation.md#second-order-motion)). There the motion error is the force error: the part from A(Ω), B(Ω), M and C is at most 1.7% of the scale. Two bodies and the free-surface wave of the motion are covered by unit tests only. Quadratic damping (`BQDR`) is not used; see [Second-order motion](input_files.md#second-order-motion).

### QTFs, quadratic part

- The same mesh guidance as for near-field mean drift applies, with `velocity_method = source` on hulls with sharp edges, as for the mean drift. On a sharp cylinder at 448 panels both QTFs are within about one point of Nemoh's quadratic part, and their median error against an exact reference is 0.1% to 9% ([validation](second_order_validation.md#quadratic-qtfs)).

### QTFs, potential part

**Memory.** The store also holds ψ at every Ω (6 × the number of bodies complex values per panel), the potential part of every pair, and with `qtf_b2 = control` the fields at the points of C and F. It is allocated at set-up, and the log gives its size: `QTF: with psi and the potential part the store takes <size> MB`, with the share of C and F for `qtf_b2 = control`. Measured on the sharp cylinder with one heading and 101 frequencies, with `qtf_b2 = body` (no C and F share): 8.1 MB at 448 panels and 29.4 MB at 1792. With the default `qtf_b2 = control` the store is larger by the fields on C and F. With `qtf_direct = 1` it also holds T_dir and, with `write_terms` 1 or 2, the two parts of it (see the direct method below).

**B2 and the control surface.** With the default `qtf_b2 = control`, each run also takes one field pass over the points of C and F per kept frequency (the total velocity, all headings) and one per difference frequency (∇ψ_k of every mode). The pass costs about N_hull × N_C Green function evaluations [derived: one influence row of the hull per point]. With the default `cs_*` keys, the margins and the panel size of C come from the median hull panel size, so C moves in and is refined with the hull, and this cost grows faster than the first-order solve. Measured on the free sharp cylinder (Snellius, 32 threads, 101 frequencies, 30 difference frequencies): the B2 pass took 6.5 s per frequency at 448 panels (1416 points on C and F), 69 s at 1792 (3212 points) and 288 s at 4032 (5648 points). The whole run took 15 min 34 s at 448 panels and 2 h 49 min at 1792; at 4032 it took 11 h 52 min, and with C held fixed 3 h 58 min.

- **Keep the defaults** for accuracy. B2 converges with the hull mesh and C together ([validation](second_order_validation.md#qtf-potential-part)).

- **To limit the cost** on a fine hull, hold C fixed: set `cs_margin`, `cs_bottom_margin` and `cs_panel_size` explicitly, for instance to the values the default gives on a coarser mesh. On the free sharp cylinder at 1792 panels, C and F of the 448-panel default (7.84, 7.84 and 1.96 m) took the run from 2 h 49 min to 1 h 18 min, with B2 within 6.9 / 13.8 / 6.9% (F_x / F_z / M_y) of the exact reference against 7.3 / 14.2 / 7.1% with the default. With the same C at 4032 panels, B2 kept converging (4.1 / 9.9 / 5.3%, and 0.82 / 1.63 / 1.09% away from the resonance). The default C at 4032 panels gives B2 within 4.75 / 10.42 / 5.40% and took 11 h 52 min, so the fixed C takes about a third of the time for the same accuracy. This is an option for cost, not needed for accuracy.

- `qtf_edge_weights` stays `off`: see [what was tried](second_order_validation.md#tried-and-not-kept).

**Lightly damped resonances.** A free body with radiation damping only can have a sharp resonance. On the free sharp cylinder the coupled surge-pitch mode at 0.77 rad/s has a peak of about 11 m/m in surge. There the computed surge and pitch are 3% to 5.5% off at 1792 panels, and the QTF, which is quadratic in the motions, inherits about twice that at the pairs with one frequency in the peak ([validation](second_order_validation.md#the-resonance-and-the-motions)). The second-order terms themselves are within 1% to 2% away from it. Where viscous damping matters, as at a sharp bilge, add it as external linear damping in `Hydrostatic.in` (lines 11 to 16): it lowers the peak and with it this error. Measured on the free sharp cylinder with external damping in heave and pitch of 1.4 and 2.1 times the radiation damping at the 0.77 rad/s peak: the potential part was within about 3% of the exact reference over all pairs at 1792 panels, against up to 13% without the damping ([validation](second_order_validation.md#damped-free-body)). Model tests of a floating wind turbine also found its low-frequency resonant response sensitive to viscous damping (Zhang et al. 2026).

**Direct method (`qtf_direct = 1`).** The potential part can also be had by solving the second-order boundary-value problem for the diffracted and radiated potential φ_D at each difference frequency, with the body forcing (`qtf_terms = body`) or with the body forcing and the free-surface forcing (`qtf_terms = full`, below), and integrating φ_I2 + φ_D over the hull. It is written to `QTF_Direct.12d` ([Output Files](output_files.md#wamit-format)) beside `QTF_Indirect.11d`, which stays. It is a check of the indirect result. It is not a more accurate alternative on a sharp hull: both methods integrate B2 on the hull there, with different discretisations. The two loads are equal by Green's theorem to the mesh error ([Developer Documentation](developer_documentation.md#direct-method)).

- **Cost.** The solve runs after the frequency loop, at every difference frequency within `qtf_max_dw`, including those on the main grid (`RadiationOnlySolves` then covers every Ω). A difference frequency that is not on the main grid has its matrix from the radiation-only solve that ψ needs anyway, so the direct method adds only the right sides and one block solve for all its pairs. One that is on the main grid costs one more matrix assembly and factorisation. The cost per pair grows with the square of the number of panels. With `ISOLV = 2` every Ω also gets an O(N³) LU factorisation, on top of the iterative solve. Measured: 7.2 ms per pair on a laptop with 8 threads on the 448-panel sharp cylinder, 5% of that run (5130 pairs); the 4032-panel sharp run on Snellius (24 CPUs, 101 frequencies, 30 difference frequencies) took 2 h 26 min in all ([validation](second_order_validation.md#direct-method-against-indirect-method)).

- **Irregular frequencies.** `If_remove_irr_freq = 1` works with the direct method, if the mesh has lid points; without them the run stops. On the sharp cylinder the lid removes the spike of the direct-indirect difference at the irregular frequency ([validation](second_order_validation.md#irregular-frequency-removal)).

- **Accuracy.** On the rounded hull the direct and indirect loads agree to 0.23% (forces) and 0.35% (moments) at 4320 panels. On the sharp cylinder with the default meshes the direct load is closer to the exact potential part than the indirect one with `qtf_b2 = body` (F_x / F_z / M_y 13 / 13 / 28% against 21 / 21 / 49% at 4032 panels, largest over the pairs, resonance included). The default indirect method, `qtf_b2 = control`, is closer still: 6.9 / 9.1 / 5.8% on the same pairs ([validation](second_order_validation.md#free-body-the-hull-and-control-surface-forms-of-b2)). For accuracy on a sharp hull use the default `.11d`, and `.12d` as an independent check.

  - **Free-surface mesh.** With a generated inner mesh the panel size follows the shortest wave of the sum, λ(Ω_max)/10, so the inner mesh and the setup per Ω are much larger than for the difference alone; the log gives the numbers (`QTF direct free surface: ...`). With `qtf_fs_panel` or a `FSInnerMesh.pnl` the mesh is the one of the difference run and the sum is less accurate (about 1.5% of max |T⁺_pot| at Ω = 1.4 rad/s on the 448-panel cylinder, against 0.6% with the mesh built for the sum; test `direct_fs_sum_equals_indirect`) [measured]. Against the exact sum QTF of the sharp truncated cylinder (sharp m2, fixed and free), the free-surface part of `.12s` is within 2.0% (max) and 0.5% (median) of the exact one at every sum frequency up to 2.8 rad/s. This needs the near-waterline fit of the inner fields (commit 47d5911), which is always on; before it the error grew with Ω⁺ to a median of 17% at 2.8 rad/s. The log line `QTF free surface: N inner points within one waterline segment of a waterline take the fit ...` gives the points fitted and any left on the stencil because another waterline is close ([validation](second_order_validation.md#snellius-validation-of-the-sum-frequency)).

  - **Accuracy, as far as measured.** On the laptop the direct sum agrees with the indirect sum of the same run to the mesh error of the hull: on the sharp cylinder the gap, as a share of max |P_BODY|, is 8.8 / 5.9 / 4.5% at 112 / 448 / 1008 panels (the same rate as the difference, so a mesh error at the sharp edge); on the rounded hull with a coarse bottom it is 12.7% in the moments (1.6% in the forces) on the laptop, and 9.8 / 2.7 / 1.2% (max, all pairs) at 480 / 1920 / 4320 panels on Snellius, so it is mesh error. Against the exact sum-frequency reference of the sharp cylinder, `.12s` is within 5.4 / 5.0 / 15.3% (F_x / F_z / M_y, max, about 1% median) at 4032 panels, away from the surge-pitch resonance; with the near-waterline fit the free-surface part is within 2% (max) of the exact one and the fixed-body `.12s` within 4.4% (sharp m2; Snellius, 2026-11-18). See the [validation](second_order_validation.md#direct-method-at-the-sum-frequency).

- **Free-surface forcing (`qtf_terms = full`).** The free surface z = 0 outside the waterlines forces φ_D through Q_D, the quadratic terms of the second-order free-surface condition (the same Q_D as in the indirect method). With `full` the solve adds the term (1/g) ∬ G Q_D dS over the free surface to its right side, at every hull panel and lid point, so `.12d` is `.10d` plus the whole load of φ_I2 + φ_D, and equals `.11d` of `qtf_terms = full` (with `qtf_b2 = body`) to the mesh error ([Developer Documentation](developer_documentation.md#direct-method)). The free surface is the one of the indirect method: the inner mesh to the circle R (`qtf_fs_*` keys, or `FSInnerMesh.pnl`) and, beyond R, partial waves. It needs a finite depth, `qtf = 1` or `3`, and the memory and runtime of both `full` and the direct solve. Use it to check `.11d` of `full` with an independent route, or when the free-surface part of φ_D is wanted on its own: with `write_terms` 1 or 2 `QTF_DirectTerms.txt` gives it as the third part D_FS. The setup per Ω builds the Green function from every hull panel and lid point to every inner panel, and its Graf amplitudes for the outer part; it stores 16 · (rows) · (inner panels + (2M + 1)(N_ev + 1)) bytes for them, which the log gives (`QTF direct free surface: ... MB`), and the near-panel integrals cost up to 64 Green function calls per near pair, so on a production hull the setup is under 20 s per Ω at 4032 rows (1.3 s at 448 rows, 24 threads, Snellius) and takes 0.8 GB at 4032 rows and 360 inner panels. Per pair the outer moments of Q_D take 0.44 s on average (50 to 220 s for the 142 to 200 pairs of one Ω on the sharp cylinder, whatever the hull mesh; 1.9 to 2.5 s for two pairs on the 480-panel hull in 100 m of water, 8 threads, laptop) and the contraction under 0.2 s. The number of evanescent modes grows like h/(R − r_x), so a small hull in deep water, or a `qtf_fs_radius` close to the hull, needs far more memory: the log gives `QTF direct free surface: orders M, N_ev up to ..., about ... MB per Omega` before the frequency loop, and the run stops with a message if the arrays do not fit. The test cases run in 35 to 71 s on a quiet laptop with 8 threads (up to 134 s under load; 480 panels, depth 20 m). The cost per Ω is measured on Snellius in the [validation](second_order_validation.md#direct-method-with-the-free-surface-forcing). With field points (`qtf_field = 1`) the free-surface part of φ_D is solved apart from the body part, so the direct columns of `QTF_Field.txt` stay the body part (φ_I2 + the body-forced φ_D, as its header says) and `.12d` still has the whole load.

### Second-order motion

- **The static diagonal and mooring.** On the difference diagonal (ω_i = ω_j) Ω = 0 and the equation is static: (C + K_ext) ξ⁽²⁾ = F̄, the offset under the mean drift force. For a body without mooring, C + K_ext has no restoring in surge, sway and yaw and is singular. The run checks the matrix of each Ω by its reciprocal condition number rcond (LAPACK `ZGECON`, 1-norm). If rcond < 1e-12, the pairs of that Ω are left out of the motion files, the file header names them (`# Left out: ...`), and the log has one line, for an unmoored body:
  ```
   QTF motion: Omega =   0.00000 rad/s, rcond 0.000E+00 < 1e-12 (modes 1 2 6 without restoring); its pairs are left out of the QTF_Motion files.
  ```
  Every other Ω gets one line ` QTF motion: Omega = ... rad/s, rcond ...` (`qtf_motion_part`, [QTFDriver.f90](../src/QTFDriver.f90)). To get the static diagonal, give K_ext in surge, sway and yaw; heave, roll and pitch have the hydrostatic C. A spring in surge alone leaves sway and yaw free, so Ω = 0 is still left out, surge included (integration test `motion_integration_rows`: modes 2 6 named). An Ω > 0 with a small but regular matrix, such as a lightly damped slow-drift resonance, is written as computed.

- **Example: the validation case.** The free sharp truncated cylinder (radius 10 m, draft 10 m, depth 100 m) gets springs for a natural period of 100 s and dampers of 5% of critical in surge, sway and yaw, with ω_n = 2π/100 = 0.0628 rad/s: K_11 = K_22 = 1.96063e4 N/m and B_11 = B_22 = 3.12044e4 N s/m (from M + A_11), K_66 = I_66 ω_n² = 6.20126e5 N m and B_66 = 0.1 I_66 ω_n = 9.86960e5 N m s (A_66 = 0 for an axisymmetric hull). The derivation is beside `SHARP_MOTION_K11` in `pre_postprocessing_python/SecondOrder/drift_convergence.py`. In `Hydrostatic.in` they are the (1,1), (2,2) and (6,6) entries of the external restoring and external linear damping matrices. The slow-drift resonance is then at Ω ≈ 0.063 rad/s; the validation grid adds 1.013 rad/s to 0.40–1.40 rad/s so that the pair (0.95, 1.013) has Ω = 0.063 rad/s. With these springs every Ω, Ω = 0 included, is in the files, and the smallest rcond was 2.6e-5 ([validation](second_order_validation.md#second-order-motion)).

- **Damping: give low-frequency damping as `BLNR`.** The motion takes B(Ω) + `BLNR` only. `BQDR` is a linearisation on the first-order motion amplitude, iterated in `SolveMotion`, and has no second-order form, so the motion does not use it. If any entry of `BQDR` is not zero, the log warns once (`body_terms_setup`, [SecondOrderDriver.f90](../src/SecondOrderDriver.f90)):
  ```
   QTF warning: the quadratic damping BQDR is not used by the second-order motion (qtf_motion = 1),
   which has no second-order form of it; the motion takes B(Omega) + BLNR. Give the low-frequency
   damping of the mooring or the hull as BLNR if it is needed there.
  ```
  The damping sets the height of a slow-drift peak: in the validation case the peak at Ω = 0.063 rad/s is about 10 times the static surge, as 1/(2ζ) with ζ = 5% from `BLNR` predicts ([validation](second_order_validation.md#second-order-motion)). Without `BLNR` the peak is set by B(Ω) alone.

- **Accuracy.** On the validation case the motion error is the force error: the part of the error from A(Ω), B(Ω) and the polygon's M and C is at most 1.7% of the scale at 448 panels and 0.03% at 1792 ([validation](second_order_validation.md#second-order-motion)). Converge the force first.

### Partial second-order field at the field points

So the omitted part comes from the bodies and falls off with distance from them, as their first-order waves do. It is small only where those waves are small against the incident wave, for example around a hull small against the wave length: on a hull of radius 0.05 m the file gives the Stokes wave to a relative error of at most 6.2e-5 ([validation](second_order_validation.md#partial-second-order-field)). Use the file for checks, and for the field away from the bodies with this in mind. The complete field, with the second-order diffracted and radiated waves, is a later phase.

**Points.** As for `PressureElevation.6p`, a point with |z| ≤ 1e-6 is an elevation point (its z is set to 0) and a point with z < −1e-6 a pressure point. The run stops with a message naming the point and the body when a point is:

- above the free surface (z > 1e-6);

- **Points.** The point rules above apply. A point on z = 0 off the hull keeps the factor 4π: the image term of G coincides with 1/r there, so the singularity is 2/r over the half space below z = 0 [derived; `QTFDirectField.f90`]. Approaching the hull, the field tends to the value on the hull panel: on the truncated cylinder it is within 1.5e-3 at 0.25 panel sizes from a panel centroid (measured in the unit tests, [validation](second_order_validation.md#direct-part-of-the-field)).

- **Finite depth.** The direct part at the points uses the Green function without the Limes correction, as the first-order field at the same points does, so isolated outliers of the series do not land on single points. At depth 10 on a truncated cylinder this differs from the route of the hull solve by 1.1e-5 relative in the influence rows (measured in the unit tests).

### WAMIT Format

    - With `velocity_method = differencing`, near-field converges slowly on hulls with sharp edges, such as a flat bottom meeting a wall. `velocity_method = source` takes away most of that excess at low frequency, but not all of it, and it is less close than differencing on rounded hulls. See [which method to use](input_files.md#which-method-to-use), measured up to 7680 panels. Rows cosine spaced towards the waterline converge: on a free rounded cylinder on springs, near-field is 1.36%, 0.40%, 0.21% above far-field at 480, 1920, 4320 panels, at 1.10 rad/s. On such hulls the far-field method is the reference for $F_x$, $F_y$ and $M_z$ of the whole system. When both methods run, the log prints their relative difference per frequency and heading for those three loads, with $M_z$ moved to the origin. The difference has no meaning for a load that is zero by symmetry, so the values are printed with it. See [the test fixtures](../test/data/drift_cyl/README.md) for values against panel count.

- `QTF_Indirect.11d file`: the **difference-frequency QTF with the body forcing of the second-order potential, per body**, written when `qtf_terms = body` or `full` and `qtf` is 1 or 3. It is `QTF_Quadratic.10d` plus the potential part by the indirect method ([QTFs, potential part](input_files.md#qtfs-potential-part)): the same columns, rows, order, scaling and conjugation, `qtf_max_dw` included, so the two files can be read row against row. The definition of $T^-$ above holds with $T^-$ now the rows of `.11d`, and $T^-_{ji} = (T^-_{ij})^*$ holds for the potential part too (it takes $\psi(-\Omega) = \psi(\Omega)^*$, [QTFPotential.f90](../src/QTFPotential.f90)). With `qtf_terms = body` the free-surface integral of the second-order potential is left out, so the file is the analogue of Nemoh's DUOK + HASBO, not a complete QTF. With `qtf_terms = full` it is added: `.11d` is then `.10d` plus all the terms of `QTF_PotentialTerms.txt`, the complete difference-frequency QTF of the indirect method. On the sharp cylinder the free-surface terms on the default inner mesh are within 4.5% of the exact value at 1792 panels, and get more accurate with a finer hull mesh; see the accuracy note of `qtf_terms` in [Input files](input_files.md) and the [validation](second_order_validation.md#qtf-free-surface-integral). The free-surface terms are zero on the diagonal, and $T^-_{ji} = (T^-_{ij})^*$ holds for them as well (a pair with ω_i < ω_j is computed as (j, i) and conjugated, [QTFDriver.f90](../src/QTFDriver.f90)).

    - **Diagonal.** At equal frequencies (also with two headings) the potential part is zero, and the rows of `.11d` are those of `.10d`, digit for digit: the mean drift has no potential part. In finite depth the potential part does not tend to zero as $\Omega = \omega_i - \omega_j \to 0$: the second-order incident potential goes like $1/\Omega$ (the set-down under a wave group), so its Froude-Krylov load stays finite. So in finite depth `.11d` jumps between the diagonal and its neighbours. This is the physics, not an error. On the sharp truncated cylinder (depth 100 m) the exact Froude-Krylov F_z is −0.83 at (0.70, 0.71) rad/s, a downward load ([truncated_cylinder_reference.py](../pre_postprocessing_python/SecondOrder/truncated_cylinder_reference.py), `qtf-potential`). Interpolate along lines parallel to the diagonal, not across it [derived].

    - The log gives the number of difference frequencies and radiation-only solves, the size of the store, and with `qtf_b2 = control` the points on C and F and the time of each field pass. With `qtf_terms = full` it also gives the inner free-surface points, the circle R and its centre, the mode counts M and N_ev of the partial waves, the time of the free-surface fields per frequency and of ψ there per difference frequency, the coverage of a mesh from `FSInnerMesh.pnl`, and per difference frequency the number of pairs, the largest r_far and the time of the potential part (`QTF free surface integral: ...`). A ψ or a pair with NaN or Inf is logged (`QTF error: ...`); the files are still written, and the run ends with exit code 1.

- `QTF_Direct.12d file`: the **difference-frequency QTF with the second-order potential from the direct method, per body**, written when `qtf_direct = 1` (with `qtf_terms = body` or `full` and `qtf` 1 or 3). It has the rows, order, columns, scaling and conjugation of `QTF_Indirect.11d`, and holds `QTF_Quadratic.10d` plus the load of the second-order potential $\phi_{I2} + \phi_D$, with $\phi_D$ solved at each difference frequency with the body forcing and, with `qtf_terms = full`, the free-surface forcing as well (with `body` there is none). Green's second identity for $\phi_D$ and the radiation potential $\psi_k$ at the same $\Omega$ (both harmonic, with the same free-surface and radiation conditions) gives $\int_S \phi_D n_k\,dS = \int_S \psi_k\,\partial\phi_D/\partial n\,dS$, so with the same forcing the load of $\phi_{I2} + \phi_D$ equals the potential part of `.11d` of the same `qtf_terms` [derived, see [Developer Documentation](developer_documentation.md#direct-method); the two methods are Nemoh's `.12d` and `.11d`, which the design spec requires to converge to each other]. So `.12d` and `.11d` with `qtf_b2 = body` (the hull form of B2, which the direct method also uses) are two routes to the same quantity and differ by the mesh error. Measured on the rounded hull, the largest difference is 1.88 / 0.48 / 0.23% (forces) over 480 / 1920 / 4320 panels ([validation](second_order_validation.md#direct-method-against-indirect-method)). With the default `qtf_b2 = control` they differ by the difference of the two forms of B2. At equal frequencies `.12d` is `.10d`, digit for digit. If the direct solve did not run at some difference frequency, the run stops with a message instead of writing an incomplete file. The name is not WAMIT's. With `qtf_terms = body` HAMS's `QTF_Direct.12d` is the body forcing only, while a WAMIT `.12d` holds the complete difference-frequency QTF with the free-surface integral, so the two are not comparable (and `qtf_compare.py --against-wamit` does not read it). With `qtf_terms = full` `.12d` has the free-surface forcing too, so it holds the same quantity as a WAMIT `.12d` [derived; it was not compared with a WAMIT run].

- `QTF_DirectTerms.txt file`: the **parts of the load of `QTF_Direct.12d`**, written when `qtf_direct = 1` and `write_terms` is 1 or 2, after four comment lines starting with `#`. Columns: ω_i, ω_j, β1, β2 and the index $6(b-1)+I$ as in `.12d`, then Re and Im of each part: 1 `D_I2`, the load of the second-order incident potential $\phi_{I2}$, 2 `D_D`, the load of $\phi_D$ of the body forcing. With `qtf_terms = full` a third part follows, 3 `D_FS`, the load of $\phi_D$ of the free-surface forcing, so the file has 11 columns; with `qtf_terms = body` it has 9 and is as before, byte for byte. The header names the parts. They are scaled and conjugated as `.10d`, written with 16 significant digits, and `.12d` − `.10d` is their sum. `D_FS` is the direct-method counterpart of `FS_inner` + `FS_outer` of `QTF_PotentialTerms.txt` (the same quantity by Green's theorem, to the mesh error), and `D_I2` + `D_D` that of the first five terms. One row per pair with ω_i ≠ ω_j, in ascending |ω_i − ω_j|, not in the order of `.12d`: match them by the keys.

- `QTF_Indirect.11s file`: the **sum-frequency QTF with the second-order potential from the indirect method, per body**, written when `qtf` is 2 or 3 and `qtf_terms = body` or `full`, with or without `qtf_direct = 1` (`qtf_finish`, [QTFDriver.f90](../src/QTFDriver.f90)). It is `QTF_Quadratic.10s` plus T⁺_pot, the potential part at Ω = ω_i + ω_j: the same columns, order, scaling and conjugation as `.10s` (time factor $e^{+i\omega t}$, $T^+$ of the definition above), so `.11s`, `.10s` and `.12s` can be read row against row. The rows are those of `.10s` whose sum is kept, the diagonal included: pairs with ω_i + ω_j above `qtf_max_sw`, or at or above the estimate of the lowest irregular frequency with `If_remove_irr_freq = 0`, have no row in `.11s` (`sum_pair_index` = 0); `.10s` keeps them. With `qtf_terms = body` the free-surface integral is left out and the log warns that the sum is not usable; with `full` it is added. With `qtf_b2 = control` (the default) B2 of the potential part is on the control surface, with any `qtf`; with `qtf_b2 = body` it is on the hull. So the same input gives the same `.11s` for `qtf = 2` and `qtf = 3` (`potential_part_sum`, QTFDriver.f90:1994-2005; `uses_b2_control`, SecondOrderDriver.f90:891-900). $T^+_{ji} = T^+_{ij}$ holds for the potential part, so a pair and its mirror have the same row. With `qtf_direct = 1` it is the indirect check of `.12s`; the two are equal to the mesh error ([validation](second_order_validation.md#direct-method-at-the-sum-frequency)).

    - **Header.** Lines starting with `#` (`write_motion_header`, [SecondOrderOutput.f90](../src/SecondOrderOutput.f90)): the force file and its route (`indirect` or `direct`); the columns, the scaling and the conjugation; the equation solved, `[-Omega^2 (M + A) - i Omega (B + BLNR) + C + KSTF] xi = F`, with the note that `BQDR` is not used and that at Ω = 0 it is `(C + KSTF) xi = F`; then either `# Left out: none (every Omega has rcond >= 1e-12).` or one line `# Left out: the pairs of Omega = ... rad/s, rcond ... < 1e-12 (modes ... without restoring).` per Ω left out. The modes are the index I below. Unlike the force files, these files have comment lines, so a reader must skip lines starting with `#` (`qtf_compare.py --motion` does, [Python Scripts](python_scripts.md)).

    - **Rows.** The rows of the force file, in its order, less the pairs of an Ω whose matrix has rcond < 1e-12 (for an unmoored body the difference diagonal, Ω = 0). Columns `PER1 PER2 BETA1 BETA2 I Mod Pha Re Im`, as in the force files (format `4ES14.6,I6,4ES14.6`), with I = 6(b − 1) + mode and the rotations about `XR` of the body (unit test `motion_rows_follow_force`, integration test `motion_integration_rows`).

    - **With `qtf_motion = 1`** (free body): `Re_dir` and `Im_dir` also hold the wave radiated by the second-order motion, $i\rho\Omega\phi_R$ (pressure) or $(i\Omega/g)\phi_R$ (elevation), with $\phi_R = \sum_j (-i\Omega\xi_j)\psi_j$, $\xi$ the motion of `QTF_Motion.12d` (K = 1) or `.12s` (K = 2) and $\psi_j$ the radiation potentials at the point. A 19th column `has_rad` follows `has_direct`: 1 where the row has $\phi_R$, 0 where it has no direct part or where its Ω is left out of the motion (rcond < 1e-12), so that `Re_dir` and `Im_dir` hold $\phi_D$ only. The header then says `radiated wave of the second-order motion included where has_rad = 1 (Phase 6c)` in the line on $\phi_D$, has one more line `# has_rad: ...` with these forms, and its `# Columns:` line ends with `has_rad`. The conjugation of the rows is the same as for $\phi_D$. With `qtf_motion = 0` the file is unchanged, to the byte (unit test `field_radiated_off`). The radiated wave is added by `motion_field_part` ([QTFDriver.f90](../src/QTFDriver.f90)) through `qfield_add_radiated` ([QTFField.f90](../src/QTFField.f90)); $\psi_j$ at the points comes from `psi_at_points` ([QTFDriver.f90](../src/QTFDriver.f90)).

    - For I = 3, 4, 5, every `control` run also builds F and evaluates the field on it, and computes the hull velocity and the waterline potential (by `velocity_method` and `waterline_method`, as for `near`), even without `near`. There is no switch to leave them out. So a `control` run without `near` also logs the velocity fallback count (or the source solve time), and with `waterline_method = field` it also stops on the flare check and prints the finite-depth warning of `near`. Accuracy, measured on a rounded-bilge and a sharp-bilge cylinder (a = 10 m, 100 m depth, defaults of `cs_*`, `cs_velocity = green`): M_x and M_y are within max(1% of near-field at 7680 or 7168 panels, 0.001 rho g A^2 a^2) at 4320 and 7680 panels (4032 and 7168 on the sharp hull). The rounded-hull F_z at 4320 panels is within 1% of the extrapolated near-field limit, except about 2% (fixed) at 1.10 rad/s. On the 480-panel test hulls these loads are within 16% (free) and 29% (fixed) of near-field, which is itself not converged there; use a finer mesh. On a hull with sharp edges, F_z of the control-surface method is the better value. The sharp hull is a truncated cylinder (radius 10 m, draft 10 m, depth 100 m) with a semi-analytical reference, from `pre_postprocessing_python/SecondOrder/truncated_cylinder_reference.py`, whose uncertainty is 1e-4 rho g A^2 (3e-3 free at 0.70 rad/s). At heading 0 and 0.70, 0.95, 1.10 rad/s, control F_z at 4032 / 7168 panels is off the reference by 0.40 / 0.31%, 0.89 / 0.63%, 2.26 / 1.44% fixed, and 0.81 / 0.71%, 0.04 / 0.09%, 0.77 / 0.53% free. Near-field F_z at 7168 panels is 12% to 17% off fixed and 23% to 506% off free; free at 0.70 rad/s the reference is -0.6128, control -0.6171 and near-field +2.49. At 1.6 to 1.8 rad/s, F_z, M_x and M_y differ from near-field of the same mesh by up to about 30% at 1920 (rounded) and 1792 (sharp) panels; these frequencies were not run on finer meshes. F_z, M_x and M_y were validated with `cs_shape = cylinder` only. Details: [Second-order validation](second_order_validation.md#control-surface-method).

Run it with `list` to see the cases. Its `collect` step needs `scipy`, for the MacCamy-Fuchs value. With `--linton-evans` it covers the [Linton & Evans array](second_order_validation.md#linton--evans-array-four-bottom-mounted-cylinders) (`l_square_diff_m1` to `_m4`: four bottom-mounted cylinders, 256, 1024, 2304 and 4096 panels per cylinder, fixed, `drift_methods = far near`, with 16 field points; `l_square_field_m1` to `_m3`: the same meshes at 1.10 and 1.70 rad/s, heading 45 deg, with 2746 field points on a 1.5 m grid, `le_field_grid`). With `--qtf-potential` it covers the cases of the [QTF potential part](second_order_validation.md#qtf-potential-part):

With all six modes restored, the Ω = 0 diagonal is in the files. They write `QTF_Motion.11d`, `.12d`, `.11s` and `.12s`. A `stiffness` (like `damping`) is written for the `sharp` hull only. Group `M`, question `6c`.

- `q_sharp_full_free_m1` is the default inner mesh (panels λ/10 at 1.40 rad/s, 3 radial rows, 30 chords on the circle) and the R = 15 m point of the radius study (15 m is the default radius at m1, 5 m beyond the waterline at 10 m).

- `q_sharp_full_free_R20_m1`, `_R30_m1`: the radius study, `qtf_fs_radius` 20 and 30 m with the default rows of each (6 and 12).

**`SecondOrder/truncated_cylinder_reference.py`** computes a semi-analytical reference (eigenfunction matching, no panels) for the sharp truncated cylinder of the convergence study: A, B, X, RAOs, far-field F_x, and near-field F_x, F_z and M_y with each of the six terms, fixed and free. It needs `numpy` and `scipy`. Results, checks and the comparison with HAMS are in [Second-order validation](second_order_validation.md#semi-analytical-reference-sharp-truncated-cylinder). Run it from its folder:

- `check` runs the named self-checks, or all but the three slow ones (`uncertainty`, `compare_signs`, `potential_convergence`), and exits with 1 if one fails. A check that cannot run for want of data (`compare_signs` without the study's results) prints SKIP and is not counted as passed. About 10 min on a laptop (36 checks; the 9 min 45 s were measured with the 29 checks that existed before the sum checks, and the four free-surface checks of them take 1 min).

- `reference` writes the reference values at 11 frequencies, fixed and free, with their stated uncertainties, prints the convergence table and runs all 39 checks (the 7 min 11 s were measured when it ran 22). The stated uncertainties hold for this hull at the default resolution only; for any other cylinder or resolution they are `nan` (not measured), an empty field in the CSV.

- `qtf-potential ... --kernel sum` (default `diff`, whose output is unchanged) writes the sum-frequency QTF T+ at Omega = omega_i + omega_j: rows of kernel `sum` for every pair omega_i <= omega_j, the diagonal included, with the same columns, so the quadratic part plus the five terms (plus T_FS with `--free-surface`) is the reference of `QTF_Direct.12s`. The waves of the reference are 1/(-i) times unit waves, so every second-order value is multiplied by -1 to match the unit-wave convention of the HAMS files. The free-surface term uses the sum kernel with the second state unconjugated and three phase classes (k1 + k2 + K, -k1 + k2 + K, k1 - k2 + K), all positive, so the rotated contour needs no new rule. The new checks are `incident2_sum` (one wave with itself gives the second-order Stokes potential, 1e-10), `potential_sum_symmetry` (T+_ij = T+_ji, 1e-12), `fs_sum_bvp` (the direct solve of phi_D at the sum frequency equals ID + FS of the indirect terms, 1e-5, at N = 4000; at N = 1000 it misses by 2.9e-4 at (0.70, 0.95) rad/s, because the waterline strip is 4 times wider), `fs_sum_forcing_vs_incident` (the sum forcing of incident-only fields equals the sum-frequency incident wave's own, 1e-10) and `fs_sum_radius_invariance` (T_FS+ at R = 15 and 20 m, 1e-6).

- `qtf-potential ... --free-surface [--fs-radius R] [--fs-check]` adds the free-surface integral T_FS of the second-order potential, the columns `Fx_FS_re` … `My_FS_im`, so that the quadratic part plus all six terms is the complete difference QTF (the reference of `qtf_terms = full`). The integral over z = 0 is split at R (default 20 m): a polar grid inside, angular modes outside, and beyond about 2 km a rotated contour that sums the oscillating tail exactly. `--fs-check` also splits at 1.5 R and writes the relative change in `fs_check`. The sign of the term comes from a direct solve of the second-order problem (`check fs_sign_from_bvp`). The method and its accuracy are in the script's docstring, section "QTF free-surface integral". Run time at the default resolution, measured on a laptop: about 0.33 s per frequency pair, 5 s per frequency and 4 s per difference frequency on top of the run without it, so about 25 min more per motion on the `hams` grid (50 min with `--fs-check`). Runs of the full grid go to Snellius.

- `qtf-motion --out CSV [--kernel diff|sum] [--k-ext K11] [--b-ext B11] [--grid motion|nemoh|hams] [--light] [--free-surface] [--from CSV]` writes the second-order motion xi_1, xi_3, xi_5 of the free cylinder (Phase 6c, the reference of `QTF_Motion.*`): for each pair of the kernel it takes T (the quadratic part, the five body-forcing terms and, with `--free-surface`, T_FS, computed as `qtf-potential` does or read from a `qtf-potential` CSV with `--from`) and solves [-Ω² (M + A) - iΩ (B + B_ext) + C + K_ext] ξ = ρ g T with A and B at |Ω| from the radiation potentials of T, M and C of the exact cylinder, and the surge spring and damper `--k-ext`, `--b-ext` (default the 100 s spring and 5 % damper of the motion cases, `SHARP_MOTION_K11` and `_B11` of `drift_convergence.py`, [the validation case](input_files.md#second-order-motion); sway and yaw are not solved, so their springs are not needed), on the grid of the motion cases by default (`--grid motion`, `drift_convergence.QTF_MOTION_FREQUENCIES`). Ω = ω_i − ω_j for the difference (the matrix conjugated for Ω < 0, as in HAMS) and ω_i + ω_j for the sum; at Ω = 0 the equation is static. A pair whose matrix has rcond < 1e-12 gets empty `xi` fields, as HAMS leaves it out. The CSV has the columns `big_omega`, `rcond`, the total T (`Fx_total_re` …) and `xi1_re` … `xi5_im`. The cost is that of `qtf-potential` on the same grid; with `--from` it takes seconds.

- `check motion_ref_one_dof_limit motion_ref_ab_match` checks the motion solve (under 1 s; part of a bare `check`): heave alone against the one-degree-of-freedom closed form ξ = F/(c − Ω²(m + a) − iΩb) of the unit test `motion_one_dof`, at Ω = 2π/100, ±0.30, 1.40 and 0 rad/s, with surge and pitch zero (1e-12; measured 2.1e-16), and A, B at Ω against the reference's first-order A, B at ω = Ω (1e-12; measured 0).

- `compare` reads the `a_sharp_*` case folders of the study under `RESULTS` (`output/Wamit_format/`) and prints, per case and frequency, the percentage difference of HAMS from the reference. Folders that are not study cases, or have no output, are skipped with a note. 93 s, nearly all of it the reference rows; `--reference` reads them from a CSV of `reference` instead (under 1 s). The uncertainty shown after each drift percentage is the one the reference rows carry.

**`SecondOrder/linton_evans_reference.py`** computes the exact solution for wave scattering by an array of bottom-mounted vertical circular cylinders (Linton & Evans 1990), without panels: the exciting forces and moments per cylinder, the elevation at any point, the near-field mean drift per cylinder, the far-field mean drift of the array, and the mean drift on a vertical control circle. It needs `numpy` and `scipy` (and `matplotlib` for `plot`). The method, checks and results are in [Second-order validation](second_order_validation.md#linton--evans-array-four-bottom-mounted-cylinders). Run it from its folder:

- `check` runs the 13 self-checks, or the named ones, and exits with 1 if one fails (3 min 46 s on a laptop, measured once).

- `compare` reads the `l_square_*` case folders under `RESULTS` (`output/Wamit_format/`) and prints the largest error of each quantity per case: X forces and moments (complex, over the largest of each kind for that body), near-field F and M (over the largest load of the array at that frequency and heading), far-field F, far-field M_z and the elevation. `--out` writes the rows per frequency, heading and body.

- `plot` writes PNGs of the reference (the wave field, the loads per cylinder against one cylinder alone, Kim (1993), Fig. 3, and Linton & Evans (1990), Figs. 2 and 3 redrawn with the digitised values of `linton_evans_digitised.csv`, which is not in the repository: set `LINTON_EVANS_DIGITISED` to its path, or these two figures are skipped); with `RESULTS`, also HAMS in those figures and the elevation error at the field points (`plot_field_error`), as maps when the `l_square_field_*` cases are there; with `RESULTS`, also |X_1|, |X_2| and the near-field and far-field drift of HAMS against the reference, per heading, and the largest errors against the panel count.

- `--direct` compares the direct solve with the indirect one, case by case, for the `q_*direct*` cases (`qtf_direct = 1`): T_dir (`QTF_Direct.12d` − `.10d`, or the parts of `QTF_DirectTerms.txt`) against T_pot (`QTF_Indirect.11d` − `.10d`) of the same run, as |T_dir − T_pot| / max |T_pot| over the forces and over the moments (max and median), and the number of difference frequencies kept by `--max-dw`. `--by-omega` adds the largest error at each difference frequency, where an irregular frequency shows as a spike. With the CSV of `qtf-potential` the sharp cases also get `.12d` and `.11d` against the exact reference. For the `q_*_directfs_*` cases (`qtf_terms = full`, three parts D_I2, D_D, D_FS in `QTF_DirectTerms.txt`, seven in `QTF_PotentialTerms.txt`) the reference must come from `qtf-potential --free-surface` (else the case is noted `no FS reference`), and a row `FS` compares FS_inner + FS_outer of the indirect terms with the reference column FS; the free-surface columns of the direct file are D_FS. The number of parts is read from the file, with a note if it differs from the case name. `drift_convergence.py` makes these cases (`qtf_directfs_cases`: `q_round_directfs_free_m*` and `q_sharp_directfs_free_m*`).

- `--direct-sum REF` compares the sum-frequency direct solve of the `q_*directsum*` cases (`qtf = 3`, `qtf_direct = 1`, `qtf_terms = full`) with the CSV of `qtf-potential --kernel sum --free-surface`: `QTF_Direct.12s` against the exact total, `.12s` − `.10s` against the exact potential part, and from `QTF_DirectTermsSum.txt` D_FS against the exact free-surface part and P_BODY + P_FS against the potential part (the file holds both orders of an unequal pair, read in either order). A last table is the in-run check, D = D_I2 + D_D + D_FS against P = P_BODY + P_FS row by row. `check` tests it on a fixture. `drift_convergence.py` makes the cases (`qtf_directsum_cases`: `q_round_directsum_free_m*` and `q_sharp_directsum_free_m*`, IRSP = 1, differencing).

- `--indirect-sum REF` compares `QTF_Indirect.11s` of the `q_*indsum*` cases (`qtf = 3`, `qtf_terms = full`, `qtf_direct = 0`) with the CSV of `qtf-potential --kernel sum --free-surface`: `.11s` against the exact total, `.11s` − `.10s` against the exact potential part, and from `QTF_PotentialTermsSum.txt` (FK, ID, B1, B2, B3, FS_inner, FS_outer; both orders of an unequal pair) the sum of the terms against the potential part and FS_inner + FS_outer against the exact free-surface part, as errors over the pairs ω_i ≤ ω_j relative to the largest exact value. A case that also has `QTF_Direct.12s` gets the row `.11s-12s`, |.11s − .12s| over the largest |.12s|. `--exclude LO HI` leaves a band out, as for the other tables.

- `--motion REF [REF]` compares `QTF_Motion.11d` and `.12d` (with the `diff` rows) and `.11s` and `.12s` (with the `sum` rows) of the `q_*motion*` cases with the CSVs of `truncated_cylinder_reference.py qtf-motion`, surge, heave and pitch, as errors over the pairs ω_i ≤ ω_j relative to the largest exact value, with the in-run row `.11-12`, |.11 − .12| over the largest |.12|. The files hold conj(ξ) L and conj(ξ) L² (L = 1 in these cases) after `#` header lines, and are conjugated back. The pairs a file leaves out (an Ω whose matrix has rcond < 1e-12, [the static diagonal and mooring](input_files.md#second-order-motion), as the difference diagonal of a hull moored in surge only, where sway and yaw have no restoring) are not compared: the cell shows (compared/pairs), and one line per file counts them with its `# Left out` lines. `--exclude LO HI` leaves a band out, off by default (the validation uses `--exclude 0.75 0.80`, the undamped first-order surge-pitch resonance of the free cylinder, [validation](second_order_validation.md#second-order-motion)). The reference keeps the M and C of the exact circle, while the HAMS m1 polygon has M and C about 0.6 % lower, which shifts the response near the 100 s resonance by about 0.3 %. `check` tests it (`motion_compare`).

- `--against-wamit FILE` compares `QTF_Indirect.11d` and `QTF_Quadratic.10d` of each `q_<hull>_*` case with a WAMIT numeric `.12d` (difference QTF), such as `marin_semi.12d` of the OpenFAST r-test. `--hull` names the hull (default `oc4w`). The `.12d` is read as written, with the periods turned into ω and the value conjugated back like the HAMS files; `--reading` is as for `--against-nemoh`, and is never chosen by agreement. It prints, per case and file, the largest and median error over the pairs with ω ≤ `--max-omega` for three sets: all off-diagonal pairs, those with 0 < ω_j − ω_i ≤ `--max-dw`, and the diagonal (the mean drift), with the median ratio Re(HAMS) / Re(WAMIT) on the diagonal. The diagonal fixes the sign but not the conjugation, since it is real.

### HAMS-MREL

HAMS-MREL (Hydrodynamic Analysis of Marine Structures – Marine Renewable Energies Lab) is an open-source numerical tool for studying wave–structure interactions involving multiple floating bodies. It uses a Boundary Integral Equation Method to compute hydrodynamic coefficients, wave excitation forces, and related quantities for diffraction and radiation problems. The software includes OpenMP-based parallelization to improve computational efficiency and has been validated against WAMIT for a range of multi-body test cases.

### Release notes

- **Finite depth, panels closer than 2% of the depth.** Now computed with Linton's method up to ν·h = 50, which removes jumps in the Green function. Infinite depth is not affected.

- **Control-surface drift.** All six components from the first-order field on a closed surface around each body (Chen 2007). On hulls with sharp edges, use it for F_z, M_x and M_y: on a truncated cylinder at 7168 panels its F_z is within 1.5% of a semi-analytical reference, where near-field is 12% to 506% off.
