## drift_cyl

    python make_mesh.py cylinder --radius 10 --draft 10 --ntheta 32 --nz 8 --nr 6 --graded --out HullMesh.pnl

Cylinder a = 10 m, draft 10 m, depth 100 m, 448 panels, no external loads, rotation centre (0, 0, 0). `field_panels.txt` lists the hull panel whose centroid is each field point in `ControlFile.in`. The field points are written at full precision, computed from the node coordinates as written to the mesh, because the solver's on-body integrals are erratic for a point slightly off the panel.

Hydrostatics, from the 32-sided waterplane polygon, for a uniform solid: V = 3121.445 m3, Awp = 312.1445 m2, zG = zB = -5 m, M = 3.121445e6 kg, I44 = I55 = 1.815845e8 kg m2, I66 = 1.550726e8 kg m2, C33 = 3.061092e6 N/m, C44 = C55 = 7.603715e7 N m, and M15 = -M24 = M zG.

Natural frequencies, from a first-order sweep with the frequency-dependent added mass: heave 0.80 rad/s, pitch coupled with surge 0.76 rad/s. The frequencies straddle them without being at them. In a 0-degree wave, the motion per unit amplitude is about 1.8 m in heave and 0.2 rad in pitch at 0.70 rad/s, and 0.5 m in heave at 0.95 rad/s.

The irregular frequencies of the interior problem start at about 1.55 rad/s (J0(ka) = 0), above the frequencies used.

**The 1% limits of the radiated energy, Haskind and energy balance checks hold at these fixture frequencies only.** The case is a regression fixture, not a statement of accuracy over the frequency range. On this mesh, the energy balance is off by 1.3% at 0.65 rad/s, and the Haskind heave force by 1.3% at 1.20 rad/s and 2.2% at 1.30 rad/s.

On a uniform mesh refined from 384 to 3456 panels, the energy balance at 0.60 rad/s and the Haskind heave force at 1.30 rad/s converge at order 1.4 to 1.75 in panel size.

The cause of the Haskind heave error is not established. It grows smoothly with frequency (0.06%, 0.35%, 0.8%, 1.3%, 2.2% at 0.70, 0.95, 1.10, 1.20, 1.30 rad/s), while surge and pitch stay at or below about 0.18%. Two candidates remain: the panel size relative to the shorter wave, and the approach to the m = 0 irregular frequency near 1.55 rad/s.

**Sharp bilge.** The error falls slowly. Term II on the wall rows nearest the bilge holds the excess: an offline fit of the edge behaviour there, made in review and with the earlier neighbour rule, brought it to +0.9% fixed and +5.7% free at 4032 panels, 0.95 rad/s. CI pins these hulls with a regression band, `*_sharp_regression`, which holds near-field and far-field each within 1% of the recorded values. It is not an accuracy claim. The plan's 5% is run outside CI by `near_far_study.py`, and it is missed there.

**Rounded bilge, free and fixed.** Fixed and free at 0.95 and 1.10 rad/s are within 1.5% at all these meshes, which passes the plan's 5%. But free does not fall towards zero on these cosine rows: +1.2% to +1.4% at 4320 panels, and at 0.95 rad/s the difference grows with refinement.

**Rounded bilge on springs: on this mesh the difference does not fall with refinement.** At 1.10 rad/s it grows from +3.8% to +6.1%, and at 0.95 rad/s, near the heave resonance on the springs (about 0.86 rad/s), it stays at +10% to +12%. The plan's 5% is missed on this mesh family at 4320 panels. M_z on the same runs agrees within 0.11%. So `drift_round_kstf` and `external_stiffness` use wall rows uniform in z.

**Cause on both, traced by experiment: the first-order potential on the thin rows, not the drift formula and not the velocity fit.** The same hull with wall rows uniform in z converges, free and on springs. `drift_round` keeps the cosine rows:

On uniform rows at 0.70 rad/s, at 480, 1920, 4320 panels: fixed -1.04%, -0.35%, -0.19%; free +13.4%, +4.3%, +2.1%; springs -6.5%, -2.4%, -1.3%. These fall, but are not checked.

What is established, on this hull, at these frequencies:

- `CALGREEN` integrates the 1/r of a panel exactly, but takes the image term 1/r1 from one point, the centroid. On a row much thinner than it is wide, next to z = 0, that point is too close to its image. For the top row alone, the one-point value of the self term is 4.7 times the exact integral at 4320 panels (0.65 against 0.14), and 2.3 times at 480.
- The potential on the top rows is off as a result. In Fourier mode 1, with the panels of a free hull on springs at 1.10 rad/s and 1920 panels, the z derivative of the radiated part between the top two rows is 2.86 - 0.06i, where uniform rows give 1.00 + 0.61i.
- A scratch build that integrates the image term exactly over the panel, for panels within 50 panel sizes, removes this: the same derivative is 1.03 + 0.61i, and cosine rows converge (table below). Terms I, II and III then agree with the uniform rows. That build is not this code. It shifts first-order results (added mass, damping, excitation by at most 0.26% at 480 and 0.08% at 1920 panels), and it is incomplete: field points still use the one-point image, so `diffraction_matches_6p` fails on it (2.2e-2 against 1e-4). Adopting it needs a separate decision.
- Fixed bodies look right on cosine rows because errors cancel. At 480 panels and 1.10 rad/s, the exact integral moves term I by -0.10 and term II by +0.09. Terms III and IV have no such partner.
- Velocity recovery does not fix it. The fit with the known normal-offset term gives +2.7%, +4.6% at 480, 1920 panels on the springs at 1.10 rad/s, and makes uniform rows worse (-1.19%, -0.41%). A wider stencil across the thin rows gives +2.0%, +1.5%, +1.7% at 480, 1920, 4320 panels: smaller, but it stops falling, because terms I and III stay off.

Not established: why the error depends so much on the motion beyond the cancellation above, and whether all other hulls behave the same. The half ellipsoid, also thin towards the waterline, moves the same way: fixed at 576 panels, F_y from +2.8% to -0.3%. The two-body path has its own `CALGREEN`, which the scratch build did not change.

## Irregular frequency removal

**A waterplane mesh with thin rim panels shifts results under `IRSP=1`, seen on the cylinders tested here.** This is how the first-order solver behaves under such a lid, not second-order code. A hull graded towards the waterline is fine with a uniform lid, which is what `drift_irsp` and `drift_two_irsp` use: `IRSP=1` and `IRSP=0` agree there in F_x within 0.22% near-field and 0.10% far-field, against the plan's 1%.

So a ratio of shortest to longest side would warn wrongly on the first, second, fifth and sixth rows. The ratio used is capped at 1. Only rim panels count, because the fans at the centre of a disc are thin on any mesh, and edges on a symmetry plane in use are not rim, so a half lid has the ratio of the whole one.
