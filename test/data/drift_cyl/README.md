## drift_cyl

    python make_mesh.py cylinder --radius 10 --draft 10 --ntheta 32 --nz 8 --nr 6 --graded --out HullMesh.pnl

Cylinder a = 10 m, draft 10 m, depth 100 m, 448 panels, no external loads, rotation centre (0, 0, 0). `field_panels.txt` lists the hull panel whose centroid is each field point in `ControlFile.in`. The field points are written at full precision, computed from the node coordinates as written to the mesh, because the solver's on-body integrals are erratic for a point slightly off the panel.

Hydrostatics, from the 32-sided waterplane polygon, for a uniform solid: V = 3121.445 m3, Awp = 312.1445 m2, zG = zB = -5 m, M = 3.121445e6 kg, I44 = I55 = 1.815845e8 kg m2, I66 = 1.550726e8 kg m2, C33 = 3.061092e6 N/m, C44 = C55 = 7.603715e7 N m, and M15 = -M24 = M zG.

Natural frequencies, from a first-order sweep with the frequency-dependent added mass: heave 0.80 rad/s, pitch coupled with surge 0.76 rad/s. The frequencies straddle them without being at them. In a 0-degree wave, the motion per unit amplitude is about 1.8 m in heave and 0.2 rad in pitch at 0.70 rad/s, and 0.5 m in heave at 0.95 rad/s.

The irregular frequencies of the interior problem start at about 1.55 rad/s (J0(ka) = 0), above the frequencies used.

**The 1% limits of the radiated energy, Haskind and energy balance checks hold at these fixture frequencies only.** The case is a regression fixture, not a statement of accuracy over the frequency range. On this mesh, the energy balance is off by 1.3% at 0.65 rad/s, and the Haskind heave force by 1.3% at 1.20 rad/s and 2.2% at 1.30 rad/s.

On a uniform mesh refined from 384 to 3456 panels, the energy balance at 0.60 rad/s and the Haskind heave force at 1.30 rad/s converge at order 1.4 to 1.75 in panel size.

The cause of the Haskind heave error is not established. It grows smoothly with frequency (0.06%, 0.35%, 0.8%, 1.3%, 2.2% at 0.70, 0.95, 1.10, 1.20, 1.30 rad/s), while surge and pitch stay at or below about 0.18%. Two candidates remain: the panel size relative to the shorter wave, and the approach to the m = 0 irregular frequency near 1.55 rad/s.

## Near-field against far-field

Near-field F_x against far-field F_x, heading 0, as (near - far)/far. **The plan's 5% holds in CI on hulls without edges. On the coarse sharp hulls CI has regression limits, named `*_sharp_regression`, about 1.25 times the measured value, which are not accuracy claims.** `pre_postprocessing_python/SecondOrder/near_far_study.py` runs the 5% check on the sharp hull at finer meshes, outside CI.

The error on the sharp hull falls slowly, at order 0.5 to 0.9 in panel size, which is what a velocity singularity at the edge would give, since the differencing does not resolve one. At that rate it would need far more than 4032 panels to reach 5% for the free hull at 0.95 rad/s. Rounding the bilge alone removes nearly all of it, on the same frequencies, so the formula is not the cause. A floating hemisphere (no edge) agrees too: free, 1.30 rad/s, +7.1% at 256 panels and +1.6% at 1024. At 0.70 rad/s the free hull's drift force is about 4% of term I, and it is near the pitch resonance, so the tests print it and do not check it.

The fit of the near-field velocity depends on the stencil. `build_neighbours` adds panels whose centroids are within 1.5 x the panel size, which changes with the panel aspect ratio. On a=5 with bilge radius 1.5, F_x at 1.35 rad/s has these errors: +2.6%, -5.9%, -2.7%, -10.1%, -4.1% at ntheta = 24, 28, 32, 36, 40 with the same rows. With shared-node neighbours only, the same meshes give +2.0%, +1.2%, +0.6%, +0.3%. The fixtures' meshes are not chosen to be the worst of these, nor are they guaranteed to be typical.

## Irregular frequency removal

`drift_irsp` and `drift_two_irsp` use uniform meshes. With the graded meshes of `drift_cyl` and `IRSP=1`, the potential on the thin top row of the hull drops by 15% at 448 panels, next to the small rim panels of the waterplane mesh, and the near-field error grows with refinement: fixed, 0.95 rad/s, near against far is -16% at 448 panels and -144% at 1792. Far-field stays within 0.6% of `IRSP=0` in that case, because the row has little area. With uniform meshes, `IRSP=1` and `IRSP=0` agree in F_x within 0.3% for both methods at 448 panels.

The graded meshes also shift first-order results under `IRSP=1`: the heave excitation at 1.10 rad/s by 6.8% at 448 panels and 8.8% at 1792, against 0.2% on the uniform mesh at 448 panels.
