## drift_cyl

    python make_mesh.py cylinder --radius 10 --draft 10 --ntheta 32 --nz 8 --nr 6 --graded --out HullMesh.pnl

Cylinder a = 10 m, draft 10 m, depth 100 m, 448 panels, no external loads, rotation centre (0, 0, 0). `field_panels.txt` lists the hull panel whose centroid is each field point in `ControlFile.in`. The field points are written at full precision, computed from the node coordinates as written to the mesh, because the solver's on-body integrals are erratic for a point slightly off the panel.

Hydrostatics, from the 32-sided waterplane polygon, for a uniform solid: V = 3121.445 m3, Awp = 312.1445 m2, zG = zB = -5 m, M = 3.121445e6 kg, I44 = I55 = 1.815845e8 kg m2, I66 = 1.550726e8 kg m2, C33 = 3.061092e6 N/m, C44 = C55 = 7.603715e7 N m, and M15 = -M24 = M zG.

Natural frequencies, from a first-order sweep with the frequency-dependent added mass: heave 0.80 rad/s, pitch coupled with surge 0.76 rad/s. The frequencies straddle them without being at them. In a 0-degree wave, the motion per unit amplitude is about 1.8 m in heave and 0.2 rad in pitch at 0.70 rad/s, and 0.5 m in heave at 0.95 rad/s.

The irregular frequencies of the interior problem start at about 1.55 rad/s (J0(ka) = 0), above the frequencies used.

**The 1% limits of the radiated energy, Haskind and energy balance checks hold at these fixture frequencies only.** The case is a regression fixture, not a statement of accuracy over the frequency range. On this mesh, the energy balance is off by 1.3% at 0.65 rad/s, and the Haskind heave force by 1.3% at 1.20 rad/s and 2.2% at 1.30 rad/s.

On a uniform mesh refined from 384 to 3456 panels, the energy balance at 0.60 rad/s and the Haskind heave force at 1.30 rad/s converge at order 1.4 to 1.75 in panel size.

The cause of the Haskind heave error is not established. It grows smoothly with frequency (0.06%, 0.35%, 0.8%, 1.3%, 2.2% at 0.70, 0.95, 1.10, 1.20, 1.30 rad/s), while surge and pitch stay at or below about 0.18%. Two candidates remain: the panel size relative to the shorter wave, and the approach to the m = 0 irregular frequency near 1.55 rad/s.

The values in the last three paragraphs are from before the image term was integrated over the source panel. With it, the worst at the fixture frequencies are 0.77% for the radiated energy, 0.62% for the energy balance and 0.93% for the Haskind heave force.

**The field method converges to the right value, at first order. Extrapolation is at second order and closer on every mesh.** Close to the wall, the formula with constant panels returns a local average of the panel values, with no gradient. Taking the perturbation potential at the centroid of the top row, without its gradient, gives -7.2%, -3.6%, -1.8% at 1.0 rad/s and -14.4%, -7.4%, -3.8% at 1.6 rad/s on the same three meshes. That is close to twice the error of the field method. The factor is measured, not derived. The local average is the explanation we tested through this comparison, not a proven mechanism. The offset itself matters little up to 0.1.

**The 5% limit of `offset_sensitivity_recorded` is missed at 256 panels at 1.6 rad/s,** so the fixtures have 1024 panels, chosen after seeing these values.

**In finite depth, term I by field evaluation can jump with the mesh or the offset.** On 32 x 16 rows at depth 10, an offset of 0.01 gives -11.40% at 1.0 rad/s, against -1.93% at 0.005 and -2.06% at 0.05. On 48 x 16 at 1.6 rad/s, the same offset gives -4.38%, against -3.53% and -3.59%. In these runs the point is about 0.001 of the depth from the column of panels below it, horizontally, and at depth 100 the same jump comes with an offset of 0.1. The jumps are measured. The Green function also scatters where `Limes` is used, and that is measured too. No run on this branch makes the jump go away, so the link between the two is not shown here. A separate measurement on commit 36d1733, not reproduced on this branch, replaced G with a direct eigenfunction sum: term I on 32 x 16 at offset 0.01 went from -11.40% to -1.96%. In that measurement the repository's first-order results moved by at most 0.25%, and small panels at kh of 50 or more by up to 9%. A fix (Linton's method for small R/h) is planned on a separate branch and is not merged. The scan on this branch does not match the jumps by frequency: at R = 0.0098 the scatter of dG/dR is larger at 1.6 rad/s (largest move 9.87) than at 1.0 rad/s (0.435), while the 32 x 16 runs at offset 0.01 jump at 1.0 rad/s (-11.40%) and not at 1.6 rad/s. The series acceleration `Limes` is called from `PIDCOCK` in `FinGreen3D.f90`, which takes the range 0.0005 <= R/h < 0.05, and from `EIGENE`, which takes R/h from 0.05. With R moved by 1 part in 1e9, 0.0098 m from a source 0.3125 m down in 10 m of water at 1.6 rad/s, dG/dR moves by up to 10 for a median of 0.29, and dG/dz by 0.26 for a median of 11.5. In a copy of the routine with the partial sum in place of `Limes`, the same values move by less than 2e-9. The scatter falls with the depth of the source. For a source 1.25 m down it is below 1e-4 for R up to 0.3 (`t9-green-scan-limes.txt`). At R = 0.6 and 1.0 rad/s it is 1.52e-4 in dG/dR and 2.88e-4 in dG/dz, and for a source 0.3125 m down dG/dR moves by 4.50e-4. R/h = 0.06 is in `EIGENE`, which also calls `Limes`. In deep water `INFGREEN3D` shows no such jump: between the offsets of 0.005 and 0.1, term I against `extrapolate` changes smoothly on every mesh tried. The run logs a warning when this method is used in finite depth.

On the fixtures, moving the nodes by rounding size, the radius scaled by 1 + i x 1e-8 for 6 values of i, moves term I by at most 0.02 percentage points. That covers this mesh and these offsets, not other ones.

The errors are relative to the largest value of the compared quantity. Source: the test files `test/test_qtf_motion.f90`, `test/test_qtf.f90`, `test/test_qtf_field.f90` and `test/test_second_order_input.f90`.

- **A and B are those of the first-order code.** At main-grid frequencies they match `RFORCE` and `RFORCE_MULTI` to rounding. The separate whole-mesh solve of the half-mesh test differs by 3.8e-7, which is the single-precision storage of the Green function (about 6e-8 per value), not an error of the sum.
- **Reciprocity holds only to the mesh error.** `RFORCE`'s own A and B, which these equal to rounding, are asymmetric by the same amounts. So this test cannot catch a transposition; the `RFORCE` comparisons do.
- **Off the grid A and B carry the mesh error.** The same 3–5% gap is found at 0.7 rad/s on the main grid, so the gap at Ω = 1.8 and 2.8 rad/s is the error of the 448-panel mesh. The largest gap is the pitch damping B_55 at 2.8 rad/s (13%), where B_55 is small (152 against 5352 at 0.7 rad/s). Both Ω are above the lowest irregular frequencies of this cylinder (about 1.55 rad/s), and the fixture has `If_remove_irr_freq = 0`, so part of that gap may come from them.
- **The sign of the damping is pinned.** With the imaginary part of the matrix flipped, `motion_first_order_limit` fails with 4.3e-1.
